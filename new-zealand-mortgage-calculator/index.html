🇳🇿 New Zealand Mortgage Calculator

Work out a mortgage in New Zealand: enter the house price and your deposit to see the exact monthly payment, the total interest over the term and the month you become mortgage-free — with an extra-payment field that shows how much sooner you could finish, and how much interest you would save.

How to use the New Zealand Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the property price and your deposit percentage — the calculator borrows the rest.
  2. Enter the interest rate from your mortgage offer.
  3. Enter the term in years, add any monthly overpayment you plan to make, and press Calculate.
Example: A NZ$900,000 home with a 20% deposit (NZ$180,000) leaves a loan of NZ$720,000. Over 30 years at 5.5% that loan costs NZ$4,088.08 a month, with NZ$751,709.09 of interest over the term. Add a NZ$500 monthly overpayment and the debt-free date moves forward noticeably — try it above.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are New Zealand mortgage payments calculated?

New Zealand home loans use standard amortisation: interest is charged on the remaining balance (most banks calculate it daily and charge it monthly) and the rest of your fixed payment reduces the principal. This calculator uses the standard monthly annuity formula, which matches bank quotes to within rounding.

How much deposit do I need in New Zealand?

A 20% deposit is the standard banks work to. Low-deposit loans — for example 10%, and 5% for some first-home buyers — exist under the Reserve Bank's loan-to-value rules, but they usually carry a low-equity margin or fee on the interest rate until you build up more equity.

Should I fix or float my mortgage rate in New Zealand?

Most NZ borrowers fix for 6 months to 5 years at a time and re-fix at each expiry; a floating rate gives flexibility to pay extra without penalty. This calculator assumes one rate across the whole term, so it suits comparing loan sizes and terms rather than predicting the exact path of a real loan.

How it works — the formula

The monthly payment M on a loan P at monthly rate r (= annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) over n monthly payments is:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Total interest = M × n − P. With a monthly overpayment, the calculator simulates the balance month by month: interest is charged on the balance, the payment plus overpayment is applied, and the loan ends as soon as the balance reaches zero — which is why overpayments early in the term save the most.

Worked example

Example: NZ$720,000 at 5.5% over 30 years (360 payments). r = 0.00458, so M = NZ$720,000 × 0.00458 ÷ (1 − 1.00458−360) = NZ$4,088.08 per month. Total paid = 360 × NZ$4,088.08 ≈ NZ$1,471,709.09, of which NZ$751,709.09 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include application fees or legal costs?

No. Bank application fees and your solicitor, LIM report and valuation costs are separate expenses that are not part of the monthly payment maths.

Repayment or interest-only — which does this calculate?

Repayment (principal and interest), the standard for NZ home loans, where every payment reduces the balance and the loan is cleared at the end of the term. Interest-only periods exist but are limited and are not modelled here.

Is this financial advice?

No — it is an educational calculator using your figures, provided free by Loona Automation. For advice on a real mortgage, speak to your bank or a NZ mortgage adviser and confirm the figures before signing.
