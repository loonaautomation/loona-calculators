📏 T-Test Calculator

Run a one-sample t-test from summary statistics: sample mean, sample standard deviation, sample size, and the population mean you are testing against. Get the t statistic, degrees of freedom and one- and two-tailed p-values.

Quick answer: t = (x̄ − μ₀) ÷ (s ÷ √n), with df = n − 1. Sample mean 105, s = 15, n = 25, testing μ₀ = 100: t = 5 ÷ 3 = 1.667 on 24 df, two-tailed p ≈ 0.109 — not significant at 0.05.

How to use the T-Test Calculator

  1. Enter the sample mean and sample standard deviation.
  2. Enter the sample size and the hypothesised population mean.
  3. Read t, df and the p-values; compare with your significance level.
Example: A class averages 105 (s = 15, n = 25) against a national mean of 100. t = 1.667, p ≈ 0.109 two-tailed — the difference is compatible with chance at the 5% level, despite looking like '5 points higher'.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I use a t-test rather than a z-test?

When the population standard deviation is unknown and estimated from the sample — almost always, in practice. With large samples the two nearly coincide; with small samples the t-test's wider tails are the honest correction.

What assumptions does it make?

Independent observations and an approximately normal population (or a large enough sample for the central limit theorem). Strong skew with tiny samples breaks it.

One-tailed or two-tailed?

Two-tailed unless the direction was fixed before data collection and the opposite direction is meaningless in context. This page reports both so the choice is transparent.

What does df = n − 1 mean?

One degree of freedom is spent estimating the mean itself, leaving n − 1 independent pieces of information about variability.

How it works — the formula

t = (x̄ − μ₀) ÷ (s ÷ √n), df = n − 1. P-values come from the t distribution's CDF, computed via the incomplete beta function — the same values statistical software reports.

Worked example

Inputs: x̄ = 105, s = 15, n = 25, μ₀ = 100.

Steps: Standard error = 15 ÷ √25 = 3. t = (105 − 100) ÷ 3 = 1.667. df = 24. From the t distribution, two-tailed p ≈ 0.109.

Result: t = 1.667, df 24, p ≈ 0.109 — not significant at 0.05.
