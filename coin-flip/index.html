🪙 Coin Flip

Flip a virtual coin with perfect 50/50 fairness. Track heads and tails counts, percentages and streaks as you flip — or turn it into a match with best-of mode, where the first side to reach the target wins.

How to use the Coin Flip

  1. Press Flip the Coin for a single flip, or Flip 10 Times for a quick batch.
  2. Watch the running totals, percentages and streak statistics update.
  3. Optional: choose a best-of match mode first — the first side to reach the target takes the match.
Example: Two friends cannot decide who kicks off in a football game. They choose Best of 5: heads wins flips 1 and 3, tails wins flip 2, and heads takes flip 4 too — heads reaches 3 first and wins the match, fairly and finally.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the online coin flip really 50/50?

Yes. Each flip uses crypto.getRandomValues, the secure random source in your browser, so heads and tails each have exactly a 50% probability on every flip, independent of everything that came before.

Heads has come up 5 times in a row — is tails due?

No — that idea is called the gambler's fallacy. The coin has no memory: after 5 heads, the next flip is still exactly 50/50. Long streaks feel surprising, but in 10 flips the chance of at least one streak of 4 or more is actually quite high.

What is the chance of 10 heads in a row?

Each flip halves the chance: (1/2)¹⁰ = 1 in 1,024, about 0.098%. Rare for a specific run of ten — but if you flip long enough, streaks like this eventually appear by pure chance.

Maths

How it works — the formula

Each flip is an independent event with probability P(heads) = P(tails) = 1/2. Streaks follow simple multiplication: the probability of a specific run of k identical results is (1/2)k. The percentages shown converge towards 50/50 as the number of flips grows — a pattern known as the law of large numbers — while the current-streak counter simply tracks the run you are on right now.

Worked example

Example: After 40 flips you have 24 heads and 16 tails. Heads percentage = 24 ÷ 40 × 100 = 60%. That looks lopsided, but with only 40 flips such a split (or more extreme) happens about 27% of the time by pure chance — small samples swing a lot. Flip 1,000 times and the split will usually sit within a couple of percent of 50/50.

Frequently asked questions

How does best-of mode work?

In a best-of-N match, the winner is the first side to win (N+1)/2 flips: best of 3 means first to 2, best of 5 means first to 3. Sports use this format because it reduces the role of a single lucky flip.

Does the coin remember my flips?

The statistics live only in this page while it is open. Nothing is sent to a server or saved — press Reset or close the tab and everything is cleared.

Can I use this to make decisions?

Absolutely — a coin flip is a classic way to break a tie fairly, from kick-offs in sport to choosing who goes first in a game. Many people find that while the coin is in the air, they realise which result they were hoping for — which is itself a useful answer.
