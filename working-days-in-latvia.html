🇱🇻 Working Days in Latvia

Planning leave, a deadline or a project in Latvia? Enter two dates to split the period into total days, weekend days, public holidays and true working days — with the complete Latvian public holiday calendar built in, including the government's transferred days off and working Saturdays.

How to use the Latvia working days calculator

  1. Select the start date of your period.
  2. Select the end date (it is pre-filled with 31 December 2026 — change it to any date you like).
  3. Press Calculate — the breakdown appears below, including every public holiday found inside your range.
Example: From Monday, 30 March 2026 to Friday, 10 April 2026 there are 12 total days in Latvia: 8 working days, 2 weekend days and 3 public holidays (Good Friday (Friday, 3 April 2026); Easter Sunday (Sunday, 5 April 2026); Easter Monday (Monday, 6 April 2026)).

Latvia actively moves working days: each year the Cabinet of Ministers transfers working days that sit next to a public holiday to a nearby Saturday. In 2026, Friday 2 January is a day off and Saturday 17 January is a working day instead; the same swap makes Monday 22 June a day off, repaid on Saturday 27 June. This calculator knows both sides of the swap — the days off are in the holiday list, and the two working Saturdays are counted as working days. Latvia's two republic days, 4 May and 18 November, are additionally observed on the following Monday when they fall on a weekend (neither does in 2026).

How it works — the formula

The calculator walks through your range one calendar day at a time, including both the start and the end date. Each day is classified as a weekend day (Saturday or Sunday), a public holiday (from the Latvian list above), or a working day. If you enter the end date before the start date, the two are swapped automatically. Ranges can cross months and years freely.

Formula: Working days = total days in the inclusive range − weekend days − public holidays that fall on a weekday.

Worked example

Inputs: Start date: Tuesday, 1 December 2026; end date: Thursday, 31 December 2026.

Steps: The inclusive range holds 31 days. Weekend days (Saturdays and Sundays): 8. Public holidays inside the range: Christmas Eve (Thursday, 24 December 2026); Christmas Day (Friday, 25 December 2026); Second Day of Christmas (Saturday, 26 December 2026); New Year's Eve (Thursday, 31 December 2026) — 4 in total, of which 3 fall on a weekday. Working days = 31 − 8 − 3 = 20.

Result: Working days in Latvia, December 2026: 20 — out of 31 total days, with 8 weekend days and 4 public holidays.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many public holidays are there in Latvia in 2026?

Latvia has 17 public holidays and transferred days off in 2026. 13 of them fall on a weekday; the rest land on a Saturday or Sunday. Two Saturdays (17 January and 27 June) are working days instead, swapped by the government for the days off next to New Year and Midsummer. Across the whole of 2026 that leaves 250 working days.

Does this calculator subtract public holidays for Latvia?

Yes. Unlike a simple Monday-to-Friday counter, this calculator subtracts the national public holidays of Latvia automatically, using the complete official list for 2025 to 2027 — including the Easter-based holidays, which are calculated for each year. The list also includes the government's transferred days off and the working Saturdays that repay them.

What counts as a working day here?

A working day is a Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday that is not a national public holiday.

What are Latvia's transferred working days in 2026?

In 2026 Latvia swaps two days: Friday, 2 January is a day off, repaid by working on Saturday, 17 January; and Monday, 22 June is a day off, repaid on Saturday, 27 June. The calculator subtracts the two days off and adds the two Saturdays back as working days, so counts for ranges that include them come out exactly right.

Can I count working days for other years?

Yes — the calculator holds the Latvian holiday rules for 2025, 2026 and 2027, so ranges in those years (including ranges that cross a year boundary) subtract the right holidays. For dates outside 2025–2027 the weekends are still counted correctly, but holidays are only applied inside the covered years.
