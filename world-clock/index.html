🌍 World Clock

The current local time in 24 major cities around the world, updating every second. Times come from your own device clock combined with the official IANA time zone database, so daylight-saving changes are handled automatically.

Quick answer: A world clock shows the current time in other time zones. For example, when it is 12:00 noon in London in winter, it is 17:00 in Karachi, 16:00 in Dubai, 07:00 in New York and 21:00 in Tokyo.

How to use the World Clock

  1. Open the page — no input is needed.
  2. Find your city in the grid; the time and date update every second.
  3. Use the offset line under each city to compare it with your own local time.
Example: At 12:00:00 UTC on 15 January 2026 the clock shows London 12:00, Karachi 17:00, Dubai 16:00, New York 07:00 and Tokyo 21:00 — the same instant, five different local times.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the world clock stay accurate?

It reads your device's clock once per second and converts that instant into each city's IANA time zone using the Intl API built into your browser. The time zone rules, including daylight saving, come from the same database operating systems use.

Why can the date be different in another city?

Because the day boundary sits between cities: when it is early morning in Auckland it is still the previous afternoon in Los Angeles. Each card therefore shows the city's own date as well as its time.

Is this the same as the Time Zone Converter on Loona Hours?

No. That tool converts one chosen time between two zones. This page is a live board of current times in 24 cities at a glance. The existing Time Calculator on this site adds and subtracts durations — a different job again.

What if a time looks wrong?

Check your device clock first — the conversions are exact, but they start from your device's current instant. Browsers get time zone rules from your operating system, so keep it updated.

How it works — the formula

Every card converts one instant — the current time from your device — into a time zone with the built-in Intl.DateTimeFormat API and the IANA zone name (for example Asia/Karachi). The UTC offset shown by each zone is whatever that zone observes on that date, so summer and winter offsets (daylight saving) are applied automatically. Local time = UTC instant + the zone's offset on that date.

Converting a specific moment instead? The Timestamp Converter on Loona Developer Tools converts Unix timestamps to human dates and back — this site deliberately does not duplicate it.

Worked example

Instant: 15 Jan 2026, 12:00:00 UTC.

Steps: London observes GMT (UTC+0) in January → 12:00. Karachi is UTC+5 all year → 17:00. Dubai is UTC+4 → 16:00. New York observes EST (UTC−5) in January → 07:00. Tokyo is UTC+9 → 21:00.

Result: One instant shown as 12:00 / 17:00 / 16:00 / 07:00 / 21:00 across the five cities.
