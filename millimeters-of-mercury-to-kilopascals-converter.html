How to convert millimetres of mercury to kilopascals

Formula: Kilopascals = Millimetres of Mercury × 0.133322.

Factors follow NIST SP 811. The standard atmosphere is exactly 101,325 Pa; the psi is derived from the exact pound and inch. The mmHg used here is the conventional 133.322387415 Pa.

Worked example

Question: How many kilopascals is 760 mmHg — standard atmospheric pressure?

Steps: Take the value in millimetres of mercury: 760 mmHg. Apply the formula above. That gives 101.325 kPa.

Result: 760 mmHg = 101.325 kPa.

Good to know about this pair

Kilopascal: Tyre pressures outside the US are usually quoted in kPa; a car tyre is typically 200–250 kPa.

Millimetre of Mercury: Blood pressure is still read in mmHg worldwide: a healthy reading is about 120/80 mmHg.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert millimetres of mercury to kilopascals?

Multiply the value in millimetres of mercury by 0.133322: mmHg × 0.133322 = kPa. For example, 760 mmHg × 0.133322 = 101.325 kPa. The converter above does this instantly.

How many kilopascals are in 1 millimetre of mercury?

Exactly 0.133322 kPa (shown to eight significant figures). The full relationship: 1 mmHg = 0.133322 kPa, and 1 kPa = 7.500616 mmHg.

How many kilopascals is 760 mmHg?

760 mmHg = 101.325 kPa — that is standard atmospheric pressure, a handy reference for this pair.

How do I convert kilopascals back to millimetres of mercury?

Divide by 0.133322 — or use the Kilopascal to Millimetre of Mercury converter, which does it instantly.

What should I watch out for when converting millimetres of mercury to kilopascals?

Tyre pressures outside the US are usually quoted in kPa; a car tyre is typically 200–250 kPa. Blood pressure is still read in mmHg worldwide: a healthy reading is about 120/80 mmHg.
