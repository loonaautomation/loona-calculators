When is Easter Sunday?

Easter Sunday is the most important festival in the Christian calendar, celebrating the resurrection of Jesus — and the hardest major holiday to pin down, because its date is set by a lunar rule rather than a fixed calendar day.

Date rule: First Sunday after the first full moon on or after 21 March (ecclesiastical calculation). In 2026, Easter Sunday falls on Sunday, 5 April 2026.

Facts about Easter Sunday

  • Easter's date rule was set at the Council of Nicaea in AD 325: the first Sunday after the first ecclesiastical full moon on or after the spring equinox (fixed as 21 March).
  • Western (Catholic and Protestant) and Orthodox Easter often fall on different dates because the Orthodox calculation uses the Julian calendar and a slightly different full-moon table — they coincide only some years.
  • Easter Sunday can fall anywhere between 22 March and 25 April — a span of five weeks.
  • Good Friday (two days before) and Easter Monday (the day after) are public holidays in many countries, making a four-day weekend.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the date of Easter calculated?

By the computus: Easter is the first Sunday after the first ecclesiastical full moon falling on or after 21 March. Churches use fixed astronomical tables rather than the real moon, so the date can differ slightly from what the actual moon suggests. This page computes it exactly with the standard Gregorian computus algorithm.

Why does Easter move every year?

Because it follows the moon as well as the week: the full-moon condition shifts its date within a five-week window (22 March – 25 April) from year to year.

Why is Orthodox Easter on a different date?

The Orthodox churches use the Julian calendar and the older Alexandrian tables for the full moon; their Easter must also fall after Jewish Passover in their reckoning. The results often differ by one, four or five weeks — though some years both Easters coincide.

Is Easter Monday a holiday too?

In many countries (including the UK, Germany, Australia and Canada) yes; in the United States it is not a federal holiday, though some states and businesses close.
