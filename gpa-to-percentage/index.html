🔄 GPA to Percentage Converter

Grades do not travel well between countries — a 3.7, a 9.2 CGPA and a UK 2:1 all need translating. This converter handles the four most requested systems, tells you which convention it used, and converts in both directions.

Full conversion tables for the selected system are shown under the result, below the FAQs.

How to use the GPA to Percentage Converter

  1. Choose your grading system — US 4.0, India CBSE, Indian university, or UK classification.
  2. Choose the direction: GPA → percentage or percentage → GPA.
  3. Enter your value and press Convert. The convention used is named in the result.
Example: A CBSE student with 9.2 CGPA: 9.2 × 9.5 = 87.4%. A US student with a 3.7 GPA falls in the 90–92% band on the standard US scale. Same student, different languages of grading.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there one official formula for GPA to percentage?

No single worldwide formula exists — every system is a convention set by a board, university or country. That is why this converter always names the convention it applied: US band scale, CBSE × 9.5, Indian university × 10, or the UK classification bands.

Why does CBSE use × 9.5?

The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE), India, publishes the estimate Percentage = CGPA × 9.5 for converting its 10-point CGPA to an indicative percentage. It is an official approximation — subject percentages vary because the CGPA averages grade points, not raw marks.

Which Indian universities use × 10?

A number of Indian universities (including Mumbai University and several state/technical universities) publish Percentage = CGPA × 10 or a close variant in their ordinances. Others use × 9.5 or their own formula — always check your university's official conversion rule before reporting a figure.

How it works — the formula

Each system converts differently — the honest way is to name the convention:

US 4.0 scale: band-based. 4.0 = 93–100%, 3.7 = 90–92%, 3.3 = 87–89%, 3.0 = 83–86%, 2.7 = 80–82%, 2.3 = 77–79%, 2.0 = 73–76%, 1.7 = 70–72%, 1.3 = 67–69%, 1.0 = 65–66%, 0.7 = 63–64%, 0 = below 63%.
India CBSE: Percentage = CGPA × 9.5 (CBSE's published estimate); CGPA = Percentage ÷ 9.5.
Indian university (× 10 convention): Percentage = CGPA × 10.
United Kingdom: classification bands — First ≥ 70, 2:1 = 60–69, 2:2 = 50–59, Third = 40–49 — with indicative GPA equivalents of 4.0 / 3.0 / 2.0 / 1.0.

Worked example

Examples: CBSE CGPA 9.2 → 9.2 × 9.5 = 87.4%. University CGPA 8.5 → 8.5 × 10 = 85%. US GPA 3.3 → band 87–89%. UK mark 68% → Upper Second-Class (2:1), indicative GPA ≈ 3.0. Reverse: 76% → US GPA 2.0 (73–76 band).

Frequently asked questions

Why is the US conversion given as a range (a band)?

US letter grades map to percentage bands (A = 93–100, A− = 90–92, and so on on the common College Board-style scale), so a GPA corresponds to a band, not one exact percentage. We show the band and its midpoint; individual schools set their own cut-offs.

How do UK degree classifications convert to GPA?

Only indicatively. A common scholarly conversion (Scholaro-style) treats First-Class (70%+) as ≈ 4.0, Upper Second (60–69) as ≈ 3.0, Lower Second (50–59) as ≈ 2.0 and Third (40–49) as ≈ 1.0. UK marks are not percentages of a GPA scale — a 70 in the UK is excellent, not a C. Admissions offices use their own country guides.

Can I use converted grades on applications?

Use them for estimates and comparisons, but on formal applications enter your original grades exactly as issued and let the receiving institution convert them — most universities and credential evaluators (such as WES) insist on this, and an unofficial conversion can be treated as an error.
