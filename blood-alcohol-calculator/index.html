🍺 Blood Alcohol Calculator

Estimate blood alcohol content (BAC) from your weight, sex, the number of standard drinks and the time since drinking started — using the classic Widmark formula. This is a rough educational estimate only.

Quick answer: Widmark estimate: BAC% ≈ (grams of alcohol ÷ (body weight in kg × r × 10)) − 0.015 × hours, where r is 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women. One US standard drink contains about 14 grams of pure alcohol.

How to use the Blood Alcohol Calculator

  1. Enter your body weight in kilograms.
  2. Select male or female (the formula uses different body-water constants).
  3. Enter the number of standard drinks (one standard drink ≈ 14 g of pure alcohol — a 355 ml beer at 5%, a 150 ml glass of wine at 12%, or a 44 ml shot at 40%).
  4. Enter the hours since you started drinking.
Example: A man weighing 80 kg who has had 4 standard drinks over 2 hours: 56 g ÷ (80 × 0.68 × 10) = 0.103, minus 0.015 × 2 = 0.030, giving an estimated BAC of about 0.073% — close to many legal driving limits, which is exactly why estimates must never be used to decide to drive.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a standard drink?

In the US system used here, one standard drink contains about 14 grams of pure alcohol: roughly 355 ml of 5% beer, 150 ml of 12% wine, or 44 ml of 40% spirits. Other countries define standard drinks differently (the UK unit is 8 g).

How accurate is the Widmark estimate?

It is a population average. Food, body composition, medication, health and individual metabolism can move a real BAC a long way from the estimate in either direction. Only a breath or blood test measures BAC.

Can I use this to decide if I am safe or legal to drive?

No — never. Legal limits vary (0.08% in much of the US, 0.05% in most of Europe, 0.00–0.02% in several countries, and lower limits for new or professional drivers). If you have been drinking, do not drive; arrange other transport.

How fast does BAC fall?

On average about 0.015 percentage points per hour, which the formula subtracts. Coffee, food and cold air do not speed this up — only time does.

How it works — the formula

Widmark formula: BAC% = (A ÷ (W × r × 10)) − β × t, where A is grams of pure alcohol consumed, W is body weight in kg, r is the body-water ratio (0.68 men, 0.55 women), β is the average elimination rate of 0.015% per hour, and t is hours since drinking began. The result is never shown below 0%.

Worked example

Inputs: man, 80 kg, 4 standard drinks (4 × 14 g = 56 g), 2 hours.

Steps: 56 ÷ (80 × 0.68 × 10) = 56 ÷ 544 = 0.10294. Elimination: 0.015 × 2 = 0.030. BAC = 0.10294 − 0.030 = 0.07294.

Result: Estimated BAC ≈ 0.073%.

Health & safety disclaimer: Educational estimate only — not a measurement and not medical or legal advice. Individual BAC varies widely. Never drive after drinking.
