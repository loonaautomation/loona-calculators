📈 Graphing Calculator

Type a function of x — for example x^2, sin(x), 2x+3 or 1/x — and see its graph instantly. Add a second function to compare, and zoom in or out to explore intercepts, turning points and asymptotes.

Quick answer: A graphing calculator draws y = f(x) by working out y for hundreds of x values across the visible window and joining the points. This one understands x^2, sin(x), cos(x), tan(x), sqrt(x), log(x), fractions and implicit multiplication like 2x.

How to use the Graphing Calculator

  1. Enter the first function, for example x^2 - 4.
  2. Optionally enter a second function to compare in a different colour.
  3. Press Plot, then use Zoom In / Zoom Out or Reset View.
  4. Read intercepts and crossings off the grid — each square is 1 unit by default.
Example: Plotting x^2 - 4 gives a parabola crossing the x-axis at x = -2 and x = 2, with its lowest point at (0, -4). Adding the line 2x+3 shows where the curve and the line meet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What expressions can I use?

Numbers, x, + − * / ^ and brackets, plus sin, cos, tan, sqrt, abs, log (base 10), ln, exp and the constants pi and e. Implicit multiplication works too: 2x, 3(x+1) and (x+1)(x-1) are all understood.

Why does the graph of 1/x or tan(x) break in places?

Those functions jump between very large positive and negative values at an asymptote. The plotter detects the jump and lifts the pen instead of drawing a false vertical line.

How do I write x squared or a power?

Use the caret: x^2 means x squared, x^3 cubed, and x^0.5 is the same as sqrt(x). Powers are right-associative, so 2^3^2 is read as 2^(3^2) = 512.

What range does the graph show?

It starts at x from −10 to 10 and y from −10 to 10. Each zoom step multiplies the range by about 0.7 (in) or 1.4 (out).

How it works — the formula

The plotter parses each expression into tokens (numbers, x, operators, functions), inserts implied multiplication signs (2x → 2*x), and evaluates it with a recursive-descent parser that respects the standard order of operations: ^ binds tightest and associates to the right, then * and /, then + and −, with functions and brackets first. It then samples the function at 720 x-positions across the window and connects consecutive finite points, skipping gaps where the function is undefined or jumps across an asymptote.

Worked example

Inputs: f(x) = x^2 − 4.

Steps: At x = 0, y = −4 (the lowest point). At x = 2, y = 4 − 4 = 0, and at x = −2, y = 0 — the two x-intercepts. At x = 3, y = 9 − 4 = 5.

Result: A parabola with vertex (0, −4) crossing the x-axis at −2 and 2, exactly as the graph shows.
