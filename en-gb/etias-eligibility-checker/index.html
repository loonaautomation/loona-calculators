🛂 ETIAS Eligibility Checker

Answer three questions — your nationality group, where you are going, and how long for — to see which travel authorisation applies to you for short stays in the Schengen area: none (EU/EEA/Swiss citizens), ETIAS (visa-exempt visitors, once the system is live), or a Schengen visa applied for in advance.

Quick answer: EU/EEA and Swiss citizens need no authorisation. Visitors from visa-exempt countries (for example the USA, UK, Canada, Australia and Japan) travel visa-free and will need an ETIAS authorisation once ETIAS starts. Most other nationalities — including Pakistan and India — need a Schengen visa before travelling.

How to use the ETIAS Eligibility Checker

  1. Select the group your passport belongs to.
  2. Select where you are travelling and for how long.
  3. Read which authorisation applies — and always confirm on the official EU or national site before booking.
Example: A US citizen visiting France for 10 days: visa-exempt today, and in the ETIAS group once the system starts. A Pakistani citizen visiting France for 10 days: a Schengen short-stay visa is required in advance. A German citizen visiting France: no authorisation at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ETIAS, in one sentence?

ETIAS is the EU's planned pre-travel authorisation for visa-exempt visitors to the Schengen area — similar in spirit to the US ESTA — linked to your passport and checked before travel.

When does ETIAS start, and what does it cost?

The EU has phased the launch and public sources have quoted different dates and figures over time, so this page deliberately does not print a start date or fee. Check the official EU ETIAS pages on europa.eu for the current date and fee before you rely on either.

Does ETIAS replace the 90/180 rule?

No. ETIAS is an authorisation to travel; the 90-days-in-any-180 limit still governs how long you may stay. Use this site's Schengen 90/180 Calculator to plan the days themselves.

I have a residence permit or long-stay visa — does this apply to me?

Generally no: residents and long-stay visa holders follow their permit's rules, not the short-stay authorisation system. This checker covers short tourist and business visits only.

Is this legal or immigration advice?

No — general guidance only. Entry decisions rest with border authorities, and your exact position depends on your documents. Confirm with official sources.

How it works — the formula

The logic follows the EU's short-stay framework: EU/EEA/Swiss citizens exercise free movement — no authorisation. Visa-exempt nationals (the EU's Annex II list, which includes the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Japan, the UAE, Brazil and others) may enter for short stays without a visa and are the group ETIAS is being built for. Visa-required nationals (Annex I, including Pakistan, India, China, Egypt, Nigeria and Bangladesh) must hold a Schengen visa issued before travel. Stays over 90 days are never covered by either short-stay route — they need a national long-stay visa or residence permit. Ireland is outside Schengen and runs its own rules.

Worked example

Case 1: US passport, France, 10 days → visa-exempt group → travel today without a visa; ETIAS authorisation will be needed once the system is live; the stay must fit the 90/180 limit.

Case 2: Pakistani passport, France, 10 days → visa-required group → apply for a Schengen short-stay visa at the consulate/visa centre before travelling.

Case 3: Any passport, France, 120 days → over the short-stay ceiling → a national long-stay visa or residence permit is the route, for every nationality group.

Rules checked: 8 November 2026. Travel rules change — always confirm with official government or EU sources before you travel.
