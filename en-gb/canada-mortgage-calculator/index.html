🇨🇦 Canada Mortgage Calculator

Canadian mortgages compound interest semi-annually, not monthly — and that detail changes your payment. This calculator uses the correct Canadian formula to give you the true monthly payment, total interest and payoff date for any amortisation.

How to use the Canada Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the mortgage amount in Canadian dollars (the price minus your down payment).
  2. Enter the contract interest rate.
  3. Enter the amortisation period in years (25 is the standard maximum for insured mortgages), add any extra monthly payment, and press Calculate.
Example: A $500,000 mortgage at 5% over 25 years costs $2,908.02 a month in Canada — a little less than the same loan in the US, because Canadian rates compound only twice a year. The formula section explains why.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are Canadian mortgage payments calculated differently?

By Canadian convention (and law for most mortgages), interest compounds semi-annually — the quoted rate is a nominal annual rate with two compounding periods per year (called j2). Before computing a monthly payment it must be converted: monthly rate = (1 + annual ÷ 2)2/12 − 1. This calculator does that conversion; a US-style calculation would overstate the payment slightly.

What is the difference between term and amortisation?

The amortisation is the total time to repay the whole mortgage (often 25 years). The term is the length of your current contract (often 5 years) — at the end of the term you renew at whatever rate is then available, keeping the remaining amortisation. This calculator models one rate across the whole amortisation for simplicity.

What is CMHC insurance and does the calculator include it?

If your down payment is under 20%, Canadian law requires mortgage default insurance (usually from CMHC). The premium — a percentage of the loan that rises as the down payment falls — is normally added to your mortgage balance, so you pay interest on it too. The premium is not included in this calculator: add it to your mortgage amount if you want it reflected.

How it works — the formula

Canadian fixed-rate mortgages quote a nominal annual rate compounded semi-annually. The equivalent monthly rate is:

monthly rate = (1 + annual rate ÷ 2)2/12 − 1

From there the payment is the standard amortisation formula M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n) with n = amortisation years × 12. Because interest only compounds twice a year, the effective monthly rate is slightly lower than annual ÷ 12 — at 5%, the true monthly rate is 0.4124% instead of 0.4167%.

Worked example

Example: $500,000 at 5% (j2) over 25 years. Monthly rate = (1.025)1/6 − 1 = 0.004124 (0.4124%). Payment = 500,000 × 0.004124 ÷ (1 − 1.004124−300) = $2,908.02 per month. The same loan calculated the US way would be about $2,922 — roughly $14 a month too high.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make extra payments on a Canadian mortgage?

Most closed mortgages include prepayment privileges — commonly the right to increase payments and/or pay a lump sum of 10–20% of the original balance each year without penalty. The overpayment field shows what extra monthly payments would do; check your contract's privileges before relying on them.

What is the Canadian stress test?

Federally regulated lenders must check that you could still afford payments at a qualifying rate (your contract rate + 2%, or a floor rate, whichever is higher) — the B-20 'stress test'. It affects how much you can borrow, not the payment maths shown here.

Is this financial advice?

No — it is a free educational calculator. Rates, rules and insurance premiums change; confirm figures with your lender or a licensed Canadian mortgage broker before committing.
