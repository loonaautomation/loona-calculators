🏆 Tournament Bracket Generator

Enter the entrants in seed order (best seed first) and generate a single-elimination bracket built the way real tournaments do it: seed 1 meets the lowest seed, byes go to the top seeds, and winners flow towards the final.

Quick answer: A single-elimination bracket needs a power-of-two size (4, 8, 16, 32). With 10 entrants the bracket is 16 slots: the top 6 seeds receive first-round byes, and seeds are paired 1 vs 16, 8 vs 9, 5 vs 12 and so on so the best seeds cannot meet early.

How to use the Tournament Bracket Generator

  1. Enter entrants one per line, in seed order — strongest first (2 to 32).
  2. Press Generate Bracket.
  3. Read round 1 (and who has a bye), then the path each winner takes to the final.
Example: 8 entrants seeded 1–8 produce quarter-finals: 1 vs 8, 4 vs 5, 2 vs 7, 3 vs 6 — so if the higher seed always wins, the semi-finals are 1 vs 4 and 2 vs 3, and the final is 1 vs 2.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is seeding, in plain words?

Ranking entrants before the draw so the strongest are spread apart. Proper seeding guarantees the top two cannot meet before the final, the top four not before the semi-finals, and so on — the reward for being ranked highly.

How do byes work with, say, 10 entrants?

The bracket rounds up to 16 slots, leaving 6 empty slots. Empties are placed opposite the top seeds, so seeds 1–6 skip round 1 (a bye) and the remaining 4 entrants play the only first-round matches: 7 vs 10 and 8 vs 9 under standard pairing.

What order are the first-round matches shown in?

In bracket order — the order in which winners feed the next round. The pairing pattern (1 vs 16, 8 vs 9, 5 vs 12, 4 vs 13, 6 vs 11, 3 vs 14, 7 vs 10, 2 vs 15 for 16 slots) is the standard recursive seeding pattern used by tournament organisers.

Can I shuffle instead of seeding?

If you want a random draw rather than seeds, list the names in any order — or run them through the List Randomiser first, then paste the shuffled order here as the seed list.

How it works — the formula

Bracket size B = the smallest power of two ≥ the number of entrants N. Byes = B − N, awarded to the top seeds. The seed order for the bracket is generated recursively: start [1, 2]; repeatedly replace each seed s in the order with the pair (s, 2k + 1 − s) where 2k is the new size, so every adjacent pair sums to B + 1 in round 1. Adjacent pairs (positions 1–2, 3–4, …) form the first-round matches, and winners of adjacent matches meet next round — which is exactly what protects top seeds from early meetings.

Worked example

Inputs: 8 seeds.

Steps: Start [1, 2]. Double to 4: (1, 4), (2, 3) → [1, 4, 2, 3]. Double to 8: (1, 8), (4, 5), (2, 7), (3, 6) → [1, 8, 4, 5, 2, 7, 3, 6]. Adjacent pairs: 1v8, 4v5, 2v7, 3v6.

Result: Quarter-finals 1 vs 8, 4 vs 5, 2 vs 7, 3 vs 6; favourites' path gives semis 1 vs 4, 2 vs 3 and final 1 vs 2.
