Enter an angle and get all six trig ratios — sin, cos, tan, csc, sec and cot — in degrees or radians. Or work backwards: give a ratio to the inverse functions and find the angle.

How to use the Trigonometry Calculator

  1. If you know the angle, keep the first mode: enter the angle, choose degrees or radians, and press Calculate ratios to get all six trig ratios.
  2. If you know a ratio and need the angle, switch to the inverse mode, pick sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ or tan⁻¹, enter the value, and press Find the angle.
  3. For the angles used in every textbook (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°), use the exact-values table in the formula section below.
Example: For 30° — sin = 0.5, cos ≈ 0.8660, tan ≈ 0.5774, csc = 2, sec ≈ 1.1547, cot ≈ 1.7321. And sin⁻¹(0.5) = 30°, which is how the inverse mode gets you back.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are sin, cos and tan, in simple words?

They are ratios between the sides of a right-angled triangle. For an angle θ: sin θ = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, and tan θ = opposite ÷ adjacent. The other three ratios are their flips: csc = 1 ÷ sin, sec = 1 ÷ cos, cot = 1 ÷ tan.

Why is tan 90° shown as Undefined?

Because tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ, and cos 90° = 0. Dividing by zero has no answer, so tan 90° is genuinely undefined — the same goes for csc 0° and sec 90°. The calculator says so honestly instead of showing a huge wrong-looking number.

Should I use degrees or radians?

Degrees are the everyday unit (a right angle is 90°) and what most school work uses. Radians are the science unit (a right angle is π/2 ≈ 1.5708). The calculator converts for you and shows the angle in both units.

How it works — the formulas

For an angle θ in a right-angled triangle, with hypotenuse H, opposite side O and adjacent side A:

sin θ = O ÷ H   cos θ = A ÷ H   tan θ = O ÷ A
csc θ = 1 ÷ sin θ   sec θ = 1 ÷ cos θ   cot θ = 1 ÷ tan θ

The inverse functions run the other way: sin⁻¹ takes a ratio and returns the angle whose sine it is. To convert, degrees = radians × 180 ÷ π, and radians = degrees × π ÷ 180.

Worked example

Example: A ladder leans on a wall at 60° to the ground. Its foot is 2 m from the wall; how long is the ladder? cos 60° = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, so hypotenuse = 2 ÷ cos 60° = 2 ÷ 0.5 = 4 m.

Frequently asked questions

How do I remember the sin, cos and tan ratios?

Use SOH-CAH-TOA: Sin = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse, Cos = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse, Tan = Opposite ÷ Adjacent. It is the memory trick used in classrooms worldwide.

Why does sin⁻¹(0.5) give 30° when other angles also have sine 0.5?

Sine repeats: 150° also has sine 0.5, as does 390°. Calculators return the principal value — the standard single answer in the range −90° to 90° for sin⁻¹. If your problem needs another angle, use the symmetry: for sine, 180° − the principal angle has the same value.

Why can I not enter a value above 1 for sin⁻¹ or cos⁻¹?

A sine or cosine can never be bigger than 1 or smaller than −1, because a triangle's opposite or adjacent side can never be longer than its hypotenuse. So there is no angle with sin θ = 2, and the calculator will ask you for a value between −1 and 1. Tan⁻¹ accepts any value.
