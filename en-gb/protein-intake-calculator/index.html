🥩 Protein Intake Calculator

Work out how much protein to eat per day from your body weight, activity level and goal — using grams-per-kilogram ranges from nutrition guidance, rather than a percentage of calories.

Quick answer: Daily protein = body weight (kg) × a g/kg factor: 0.8 g/kg is the adult RDA minimum, 1.2–1.6 g/kg suits active people, and 1.6–2.2 g/kg is the evidence-based range for building muscle. A 70 kg active person targets about 84–112 g per day.

How to use the Protein Intake Calculator

  1. Enter your body weight in kilograms.
  2. Choose your activity/lifestyle and your goal.
  3. Read your daily protein range in grams, plus the midpoint target.
Example: 70 kg, active lifestyle, building muscle: the range is 70 × 1.6 to 70 × 2.2 = 112–154 g of protein per day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from the Macro Calculator?

The Macro Calculator splits a calorie target into protein, carbs and fat percentages. This page starts from body weight and goal using g/kg ranges — the method used in sports-nutrition research and dietetic practice — so the two pages answer the question from different directions.

What is the official minimum?

The RDA for adults is 0.8 g per kg of body weight per day — a minimum to avoid deficiency for sedentary adults, not an optimum for training, dieting or older age.

Why is protein higher when losing weight?

In a calorie deficit, higher protein (often 1.6–2.4 g/kg) helps preserve muscle while fat is lost, which is why the fat-loss ranges here sit above the maintenance ranges.

Can too much protein harm kidneys?

In healthy adults, intakes in these ranges have not been shown to damage healthy kidneys, but anyone with kidney disease or another medical condition must follow their doctor's advice, not a calculator. This page is educational only.

How it works — the formula

Protein (g/day) = weight (kg) × factor. Factors used: sedentary 0.8 (the adult RDA); active 1.2–1.6; building muscle 1.6–2.2 (the range supported by ISSN position stands and meta-analyses); fat loss 1.6–2.4; endurance 1.2–1.6; older adults 1.0–1.2 (PROT-AGE guidance). Single-value categories are shown as a point target.

Worked example

Inputs: 70 kg, building muscle.

Steps: Low end: 70 × 1.6 = 112 g. High end: 70 × 2.2 = 154 g. Midpoint = 133 g.

Result: 112–154 g per day, midpoint 133 g.

Health disclaimer: Educational estimate only — not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. Always consult a qualified health professional about your own or your child’s health.
