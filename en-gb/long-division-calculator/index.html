➗ Long Division Calculator

Divide any two numbers and see the complete long division working — every bring-down, multiply and subtract step, written out the way it is taught in school. Get the answer as a remainder or carried into decimals.

Quick answer: Long division breaks a big division into digit-by-digit steps: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down. For example 945 ÷ 7 = 135 exactly: 9÷7 = 1 remainder 2, 24÷7 = 3 remainder 3, 35÷7 = 5 remainder 0.

How to use the Long Division Calculator

  1. Enter the dividend (the number being divided).
  2. Enter the divisor (the number you divide by).
  3. Press Divide and read the quotient, the remainder, and the full step-by-step working.
  4. Decimal answers are carried to 6 decimal places, with a note if the decimal repeats.
Example: 945 ÷ 7: step 1, 9 ÷ 7 = 1, remainder 2. Bring down 4 → 24 ÷ 7 = 3, remainder 3. Bring down 5 → 35 ÷ 7 = 5, remainder 0. Answer: 135 exactly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does each long division step do?

Each step takes the current number (the remainder so far with the next digit brought down), finds how many whole times the divisor fits, writes that digit in the quotient, multiplies back, and subtracts to get the new remainder.

How are decimals handled?

When the whole-number digits run out and a remainder is left, the calculator adds a decimal point to the quotient and keeps bringing down zeros, for up to 6 decimal places. If the remainder starts repeating, it notes that the decimal repeats.

Can the divisor be a decimal?

Yes. Both numbers are scaled by the same power of ten to make the divisor a whole number first — the quotient is unchanged, exactly as taught in class (for example 7.5 ÷ 2.5 becomes 75 ÷ 25 = 3).

Why show the remainder as well as the decimal?

Many school courses (and real tasks like sharing items) need the whole-number answer with a remainder, while others need the decimal. This calculator shows both from the same working.

How it works — the formula

Long division processes the dividend one digit at a time. At each step: quotient digit = floor(current ÷ divisor), product = quotient digit × divisor, new remainder = current − product, then the next digit is brought down to form the next current value. Decimal inputs are first scaled by a power of 10 so both numbers are whole — multiplying dividend and divisor by the same number never changes the quotient.

Worked example

Inputs: 100 ÷ 6.

Steps: 10 ÷ 6 = 1, product 6, remainder 4. Bring down 0 → 40 ÷ 6 = 6, product 36, remainder 4. Whole part is 16 remainder 4. Continue into decimals: bring down 0 → 40 ÷ 6 = 6 remainder 4 again — the same remainder repeats, so the decimal repeats.

Result: 100 ÷ 6 = 16 remainder 4, or 16.666667 rounded to 6 decimal places (16.6 recurring).
