🌙 Moon Phase Calculator

Enter any date to see what the moon looks like on that night: its phase name, how much of its face is lit, its age in the lunar cycle, and how many days remain until the next new moon and full moon.

Quick answer: The moon cycles every 29.53 days. Its age is counted from the last new moon: around day 7.4 it is a first quarter, day 14.8 full, day 22.1 last quarter. Illumination ≈ (1 − cos(2π × age ÷ 29.53)) ÷ 2.

How to use the Moon Phase Calculator

  1. Enter the date you are interested in (today is filled in).
  2. Read the phase name, illumination, moon age, and countdowns to the next full and new moon.
Example: On 8 November 2026 at midday UTC the moon's age is about 28.1 days — a waning crescent only about 2% lit, roughly 1.4 days before the next new moon.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this calculator?

It uses the standard synodic-month approximation anchored on the known new moon of 6 January 2000 (18:14 UTC). Phase names and illumination are typically accurate to within about half a day / a few percent — fine for planning, photography and curiosity, not for astronomy-grade work.

Why does the same phase look flipped in the southern hemisphere?

The phase itself is identical worldwide at the same instant; only its apparent orientation rotates. In the southern hemisphere the lit side appears on the left where northern viewers see it on the right.

What is a blue moon or supermoon?

A blue moon is the second full moon inside one calendar month — a naming quirk, not a colour. A supermoon is a full moon near the moon's closest approach to Earth (perigee), which this simple cycle calculator does not model.

Does the moon phase affect tides or sleep?

Tides follow the moon (and sun) gravitationally and are strongest near new and full moons. Claims about sleep and behaviour are much weaker scientifically — the calculator sticks to astronomy.

How it works — the formula

Moon age = (date − reference new moon) mod 29.530588853 days, with the reference new moon of 6 Jan 2000, 18:14 UTC. Illumination = (1 − cos(2π × age ÷ 29.530588853)) ÷ 2. Phase names divide the cycle into 8 equal parts of about 3.69 days: New, Waxing Crescent, First Quarter, Waxing Gibbous, Full, Waning Gibbous, Last Quarter, Waning Crescent.

Worked example

Input: 8 Nov 2026, 12:00 UTC.

Steps: Days since 6 Jan 2000 18:14 UTC ≈ 9,802.74. Divide by 29.530588853: 331 full cycles (9,774.63 days) with remainder ≈ 28.1 days — the moon's age. Illumination = (1 − cos(2π × 28.1 ÷ 29.53)) ÷ 2 = (1 − cos(342.9°)) ÷ 2 ≈ (1 − 0.956) ÷ 2 ≈ 0.022 → 2%.

Result: Waning crescent, about 2% lit, next new moon in about 29.53 − 28.1 = 1.4 days.
