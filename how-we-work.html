How We Work

Every calculation happens on your own device. Here is how, and why it is free.

Calculations run in your browser

Every Loona calculator runs entirely inside your own web browser. When you enter values and press calculate, the maths happens on your phone or computer — the values are not sent to a server, not stored by us, and not visible to us. Close the page and the working data is gone. Because the calculation is local, it also finishes instantly, even on a slow connection.

How a calculator is made

Each calculator goes through the same four steps before it appears on this site. First, the formula. We take the standard formula or official table for the job — the same one a textbook, a bank or a government guide uses — never an invented shortcut. Second, the build. The calculator is written as a small page that shows its working: most calculators on this site print the formula they use or a worked example with every step, so you can follow the arithmetic yourself. Third, the tests. Before publication, each calculator is run against examples whose answers are known independently — published tables, hand-worked arithmetic, and official calculators where they exist — including edge cases: zero, very large values, leap years, month boundaries and rounding boundaries. Fourth, publication — only when every test matches.

Where the built-in numbers come from

Some calculators carry figures that come from the real world, and each figure has a named source. The zakat calculator's nisab threshold follows the officially announced figure. Electricity bill estimates use the tariff slabs and per-unit rates published by the distribution companies. CGPA calculations use the 4.0 grade-point scale that most universities in Pakistan follow, and the page says so, because a minority of institutions use a different scale. Hijri date conversion follows the Umm al-Qura calendar — the calendar Saudi Arabia publishes — while noting that a local moon-sighting can differ by a day. Where a figure can go stale, the calculator's page states the figure it uses, so you can compare it with the current official one.

When rules change

Tax slabs, tariffs and thresholds do not stand still. We review the built-in figures when the changes are announced — Pakistan's federal budget each June is the largest single moment, and tariff and nisab changes are followed as they are published. When a visitor reports a wrong result, we re-check the formula against the source first: if the source has moved, the calculator is updated; if the formula was wrong, it is fixed and the full test set is run again before the correction goes live. Even so, results are estimates: please verify important figures independently, as our Disclaimer explains.

No account, no download

There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to install. Open a calculator and use it. The site can be added to your home screen as an app if you like, but only if you choose to.

Free, supported by advertising

All 207 calculators are free. The site is supported by Google AdSense advertising, described in our Privacy Policy. Advertising never changes a result.
