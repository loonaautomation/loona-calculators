🇿🇦 South Africa Mortgage Calculator

Work out a home loan (bond) in South Africa: enter the property price and your deposit to see the exact monthly repayment, the total interest over the term and the month you become debt-free — with an extra-payment field that shows how much sooner you could finish, and how much interest you would save.

How to use the South Africa Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the property price and your deposit percentage — the calculator borrows the rest.
  2. Enter the interest rate from your mortgage offer.
  3. Enter the term in years, add any monthly overpayment you plan to make, and press Calculate.
Example: A R1,600,000 home with a 10% deposit (R160,000) leaves a loan of R1,440,000. Over 20 years at 10.5% that loan costs R14,376.67 a month, with R2,010,400.89 of interest over the term. Add a R2,000 monthly overpayment and the debt-free date moves forward noticeably — try it above.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are South African bond repayments calculated?

South African home loans (bonds) use standard amortisation on a monthly reducing balance: interest is charged each month on what you still owe at the annual rate ÷ 12, and the rest of the fixed repayment reduces the capital. That is the annuity formula this calculator uses.

Why is my rate quoted as 'prime plus' or 'prime minus'?

Home loan rates in South Africa are priced off the prime lending rate — the rate banks charge their most creditworthy customers — which moves in step with the Reserve Bank's repo rate. Your offer might be prime − 1% or prime + 0.5%, for example. Enter your actual contract rate here; when the repo rate changes, prime, and usually your repayment, changes too.

How much deposit do I need for a bond in South Africa?

Many buyers aim for a 10% deposit, but 100% bonds (no deposit) are available to strong applicants and first-time buyers, and the FLISP subsidy can help qualifying lower-income first-time buyers with a deposit. Remember the deposit is separate from the bond registration and transfer costs, which are also payable upfront.

How it works — the formula

The monthly payment M on a loan P at monthly rate r (= annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) over n monthly payments is:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Total interest = M × n − P. With a monthly overpayment, the calculator simulates the balance month by month: interest is charged on the balance, the payment plus overpayment is applied, and the loan ends as soon as the balance reaches zero — which is why overpayments early in the term save the most.

Worked example

Example: R1,440,000 at 10.5% over 20 years (240 payments). r = 0.00875, so M = R1,440,000 × 0.00875 ÷ (1 − 1.00875−240) = R14,376.67 per month. Total paid = 240 × R14,376.67 ≈ R3,450,400.89, of which R2,010,400.89 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include bond registration or transfer duty?

No. Bond registration and transfer attorney fees, transfer duty and municipal clearance costs are separate upfront expenses that are not part of the monthly repayment maths.

Repayment or interest-only — which does this calculate?

Repayment (capital and interest), the standard for South African residential bonds, where every payment reduces the balance and the loan is cleared at the end of the term.

Is this financial advice?

No — it is an educational calculator using your figures, provided free by Loona Automation. For advice on a real bond, speak to your bank or a bond originator and confirm the figures before signing; your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments.
