How to convert kilojoules to watt-hours

Formula: Watt-hours = Kilojoules × 0.277778.

Factors follow NIST SP 811. The (thermochemical) calorie is exactly 4.184 J; food 'Calories' are kilocalories. The British thermal unit used here is the International Table BTU = 1,055.05585262 J.

Worked example

Question: How many watt-hours is 8700 kJ — an adult's daily food energy, roughly?

Steps: Take the value in kilojoules: 8700 kJ. Apply the formula above. That gives 2416.67 Wh.

Result: 8700 kJ = 2416.67 Wh.

Good to know about this pair

Watt-hour: Battery capacity is sold in watt-hours: a phone battery is about 10–15 Wh, a laptop 50–100 Wh.

Kilojoule: Food labels in most countries list energy in kJ as well as kcal; an active adult needs roughly 8,700 kJ a day.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert kilojoules to watt-hours?

Multiply the value in kilojoules by 0.277778: kJ × 0.277778 = Wh. For example, 8700 kJ × 0.277778 = 2416.67 Wh. The converter above does this instantly.

How many watt-hours are in 1 kilojoule?

Exactly 0.277778 Wh (shown to eight significant figures). The full relationship: 1 kJ = 0.277778 Wh, and 1 Wh = 3.6 kJ.

How many watt-hours is 8700 kJ?

8700 kJ = 2416.67 Wh — that is an adult's daily food energy, roughly, a handy reference for this pair.

How do I convert watt-hours back to kilojoules?

Divide by 0.277778 — or use the Watt-hour to Kilojoule converter, which does it instantly.

What should I watch out for when converting kilojoules to watt-hours?

Battery capacity is sold in watt-hours: a phone battery is about 10–15 Wh, a laptop 50–100 Wh. Food labels in most countries list energy in kJ as well as kcal; an active adult needs roughly 8,700 kJ a day.
