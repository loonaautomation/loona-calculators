🔔 Z-Score Calculator

Enter a value, the mean and the standard deviation of its normal distribution to get the z-score, the percentile rank, and the probabilities of scoring below, above, or between — the standard normal-table results without the paper table.

Quick answer: z = (value − mean) ÷ standard deviation. A value of 85 with mean 70 and SD 15 gives z = 1.00, which is the 84.13th percentile: about 84.13% of values lie below it and 15.87% above.

How to use the Z-Score Calculator

  1. Enter the value you want to standardise.
  2. Enter the distribution's mean and standard deviation (must be over 0).
  3. Read the z-score, percentile, and the below/above probabilities.
Example: Exam score 85 where the class mean is 70 and the standard deviation is 15: z = (85 − 70) ÷ 15 = 1.00, percentile 84.13 — this student scored higher than about 84% of the class.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a z-score mean in plain words?

It is the distance from the mean measured in standard deviations. z = 0 is exactly average, z = 1 is one SD above, z = −2 is two SDs below. That shared ruler is what makes scores from different tests comparable.

How is the percentile calculated without a table?

From the cumulative normal function Φ(z), computed here with the standard Abramowitz–Stegun approximation (accurate to about 1 part in 100,000) — the same numbers printed in z-tables.

What fraction of values lie within 1, 2 and 3 standard deviations?

For a normal distribution: about 68.27% within ±1, 95.45% within ±2, and 99.73% within ±3 — the empirical rule. This calculator reports the between-probability for your z as well.

Does this work for any data?

The percentile and probability figures assume the data follows a normal (bell-shaped) distribution. The z-score itself can be computed for any data set, but the probabilities only match reality when the distribution is roughly normal.

How it works — the formula

z = (x − μ) ÷ σ. The percentile is Φ(z) × 100, where Φ is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution, evaluated with the Abramowitz–Stegun 26.2.17 approximation of the error function. P(above) = 1 − Φ(z), and P(between −|z| and +|z|) = 2Φ(|z|) − 1.

Worked example

Inputs: x = 85, μ = 70, σ = 15.

Steps: z = (85 − 70) ÷ 15 = 15 ÷ 15 = 1.00. From the standard normal table, Φ(1.00) = 0.8413.

Result: z = 1.00, percentile 84.13, P(below) = 84.13%, P(above) = 15.87%, P(within ±1 z) = 68.27%.
