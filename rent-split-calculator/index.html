🤝 Rent Split Calculator

Splitting rent equally sounds fair — until one bedroom is twice the size of another, or one roommate earns half as much as the rest. This calculator splits the rent three ways — equally, by room size, or by income — so the arrangement your household agrees on is also the one the numbers support.

How to use the Rent Split Calculator

  1. Enter the total monthly rent for the whole home.
  2. Choose how many roommates are splitting it (2 to 8), including yourself.
  3. Pick the split method: equally, by room size, or by income. For the size and income methods, fill in each person’s figures.
  4. Press Split the rent — everyone’s monthly share and percentage appear in a table.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fairest way to split rent?

There is no single answer — it depends on what your household values. Equal split is simplest and works when rooms are similar. Splitting by room size is fairer when one bedroom is much bigger or has its own bathroom. Splitting by income keeps the burden proportional when earnings differ a lot. The fairest method is the one everyone agrees to before signing the lease.

How do you split rent by room size?

Measure each bedroom (length × width, in feet) to get its area in square feet, then each person pays the share of rent matching their share of the total bedroom area. A 500 sq ft room in a home with 1,000 sq ft of bedrooms pays half the rent. Shared areas like the kitchen and lounge are used by everyone, which is why most households split on bedroom size and keep the rest equal.

Should a couple sharing one room pay one share or two?

Both conventions exist. Many households charge rent per room (the couple pays one room share) but split utilities and groceries per person (the couple counts as two). This calculator works per person — to model “couple pays as one room”, simply enter the couple as a single row in room-size mode.

How it works — the formula

Each person pays in proportion to their weight. In equal mode every weight is 1. In room-size mode the weight is the size of that person's room in square feet, and in income mode it is that person's monthly income. A share is the total rent multiplied by the person's weight, divided by the sum of all weights.

Formula: Person's rent = total rent × person's weight ÷ sum of all weights. Shares are rounded to whole rupees; if rounding leaves the total a rupee or two short or over, the last person's share is adjusted so the shares always add up to exactly the total rent.

Worked example

Inputs: Total rent Rs 60,000; three roommates with rooms of 500, 300 and 200 sq ft.

Steps: The weights add up to 1,000 sq ft. Person 1 pays 60,000 × 500 ÷ 1,000 = Rs 30,000; Person 2 pays 60,000 × 300 ÷ 1,000 = Rs 18,000; Person 3 pays 60,000 × 200 ÷ 1,000 = Rs 12,000 — together exactly Rs 60,000.

Result: By room size: Rs 30,000 / Rs 18,000 / Rs 12,000. Split equally instead, the same rent is simply Rs 20,000 each.

Frequently asked questions

How should we split utility bills and other shared costs?

For bills that grow with the number of people — electricity in shared areas, water, internet, cleaning — an equal per-person split is usually fairest, whatever the rent arrangement is. You can use this same calculator: enter the bill amount as the “rent” and choose the equal split.

What if one room has an attached bathroom or a balcony?

Weight it. In room-size mode, enter that room’s size plus a bit extra — adding 10–20% to its measured area is a common way to price in a private bathroom or balcony. What matters is that everyone sees and accepts the adjusted figures before the split is calculated.
