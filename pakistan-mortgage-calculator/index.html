🇵🇰 Pakistan Mortgage Calculator

Work out house financing in Pakistan: enter the property price and your deposit to see the monthly installment on the financed amount, calculated on a reducing balance, plus the total markup over the term and the month you finish paying — with an extra-payment field that shows how paying more each month shortens the financing.

How to use the Pakistan Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the property price and your deposit percentage — the calculator borrows the rest.
  2. Enter the interest rate from your mortgage offer.
  3. Enter the term in years, add any monthly overpayment you plan to make, and press Calculate.
Example: A PKR 25,000,000 home with a 20% deposit (PKR 5,000,000) leaves a loan of PKR 20,000,000. Over 20 years at 18% that loan costs PKR 308,662.30 a month, with PKR 54,078,953.12 of interest over the term. Add a PKR 25,000 monthly overpayment and the debt-free date moves forward noticeably — try it above.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the monthly installment calculated in Pakistan?

Banks in Pakistan calculate house finance on a reducing balance: markup is charged each month on the outstanding amount at the annual rate ÷ 12, and the rest of your fixed installment reduces the balance. That is the annuity formula this calculator uses, so the installment stays the same while the markup share shrinks month by month.

How much deposit do I need to finance a house in Pakistan?

Most banks finance 70–85% of the property value, so you normally need a deposit of 15–30% of the price, plus separate cash for valuation and legal costs. Subsidised schemes such as Mera Pakistan Mera Ghar have offered lower markup and higher financing for smaller first homes, under the rules of the scheme at the time.

Is house finance in Pakistan a mortgage or Islamic finance?

Both exist. Conventional banks offer mortgages with markup, while Islamic banks offer Diminishing Musharakah home finance, where the monthly payment combines rent on the bank's share with buying that share out — the maths works out very close to the installment shown here. Rates are usually floating and linked to KIBOR; this calculator assumes one rate for the whole term.

How it works — the formula

The monthly payment M on a loan P at monthly rate r (= annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) over n monthly payments is:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Total interest = M × n − P. With a monthly overpayment, the calculator simulates the balance month by month: interest is charged on the balance, the payment plus overpayment is applied, and the loan ends as soon as the balance reaches zero — which is why overpayments early in the term save the most.

Worked example

Example: PKR 20,000,000 at 18% over 20 years (240 payments). r = 0.01500, so M = PKR 20,000,000 × 0.01500 ÷ (1 − 1.01500−240) = PKR 308,662.30 per month. Total paid = 240 × PKR 308,662.30 ≈ PKR 74,078,953.12, of which PKR 54,078,953.12 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include valuation, legal or insurance costs?

No. Bank valuation and legal fees, any arrangement fee and property or life insurance are separate costs that are not part of the installment maths.

Does this calculator do interest-only financing?

No — it calculates a reducing-balance repayment schedule, where every installment reduces the amount you owe and the financing is cleared at the end of the term.

Is this financial advice?
