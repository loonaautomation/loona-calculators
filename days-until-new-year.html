When is New Year's Day?

New Year's Day on 1 January opens the Gregorian calendar year — greeted first by Pacific island nations and, roughly 26 hours later, last by outlying US islands, making it the most globally shared countdown of the year.

Date rule: 1 January every year. In 2026, New Year's Day falls on Thursday, 1 January 2026.

Facts about New Year's Day

  • Kiritimati (Christmas Island, Kiribati) is the first inhabited place to welcome the New Year, at UTC+14.
  • The last places to reach 1 January are Baker and Howland Islands (UTC−12) — a full 26 hours after Kiritimati.
  • Celebrating the new year on 1 January goes back to Julius Caesar's Julian calendar of 45 BC, honouring Janus, the two-faced god of beginnings.
  • Not every culture's new year falls on 1 January: Chinese, Islamic, Persian and Hindu calendars all begin their years on different dates.

Frequently Asked Questions

When exactly does the countdown end?

At midnight — 00:00 local time — at the start of 1 January in your own time zone. The counter above targets your local midnight, so it is correct wherever you are.

Which country celebrates New Year first?

Kiribati's Line Islands (including Kiritimati) at UTC+14, followed by New Zealand and then Australia's Sydney, famous for its harbour fireworks.

Why do some countries celebrate New Year on other dates?

Because they follow different calendars for cultural or religious years — for example the lunar Chinese New Year (late January or February) and the Islamic New Year, which moves earlier by about 11 days each Gregorian year.

Is New Year's Day a public holiday?

Yes, in the large majority of countries — it is one of the most widely observed public holidays in the world.

What happens to the counter on 1 January?

On the day itself it celebrates 'today'; afterwards it rolls forward to the next 1 January automatically.
