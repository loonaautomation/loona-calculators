🇪🇺 Schengen 90/180 Calculator

Enter your past and planned trips to the Schengen area. The calculator checks every rolling 180-day window — not just calendar halves — counts the days used, shows how many you have left, warns about any overstay, and finds the earliest date you could start a fresh full 90-day stay.

Quick answer: The Schengen short-stay rule allows at most 90 days in any rolling 180-day period. Both the entry day and the exit day count as days of presence, and the window moves every day — it is not split into fixed six-month blocks.

How to use the Schengen 90/180 Calculator

  1. Add each trip with its entry and exit dates (both days count).
  2. Set the check date — today is the default — to see days used in the 180-day window ending on that date and days remaining.
  3. Read the maximum days found in any rolling window: above 90 means an overstay under the rule.
  4. Use the earliest full-stay result if you are planning a long new trip.
Example: Two trips of 45 days each — 1 Jan–Feb 14, 2026 and 1 Mar–Apr 14, 2026 — use exactly 90 days. On April 14, 2026 the window holds all 90 days, 0 days remain, and there is no overstay because 90 is allowed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the 90/180 rule actually work?

On any day you are in the Schengen area, count your days of presence in the previous 180 days including that day. The total must never exceed 90. The window rolls forward daily, which this calculator checks day by day across all your trips.

Do the entry and exit days count?

Yes. Under the EU rule both the day you enter and the day you leave count as full days of presence, and this calculator counts them that way.

What counts as one trip if I leave and return quickly?

Every day physically inside the Schengen area counts, however short the gap outside. Enter each stay separately and the calculator adds them all into the same rolling windows.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is guidance based on the standard EU 90/180 short-stay rule for visa-free and short-stay visa visitors. It does not cover residence permits, long-stay visas, or the special rules some nationalities have under bilateral agreements. Border authorities decide; check official EU sources for your case.

How it works — the formula

For every date D, presence(D) = the number of your trip days falling in the window [D − 179 days, D]. The rule is broken only if presence(D) > 90 for some D. This calculator evaluates presence for every relevant day — each trip day and the days around it — instead of sampling, so rolling-window edge cases are caught exactly. Days remaining on the check date = 90 − presence(check date). The earliest full-stay start is found by testing candidate start dates day by day: a 90-day stay starting on S is safe only if presence stays at or below 90 on every day of the stay, including the new days themselves.

Worked example

Inputs: Trip 1: 1 Jan–Feb 14, 2026 (31 + 14 = 45 days). Trip 2: 1 Mar–Apr 14, 2026 (31 + 14 = 45 days). Check date Apr 14, 2026.

Steps: The 180-day window ending Apr 14, 2026 starts on Oct 17, 2025, so it contains both trips in full: 45 + 45 = 90 days used. Days remaining = 90 − 90 = 0. The maximum in any window is 90, which is allowed — no overstay.

Result: 90 days used, 0 remaining, no overstay — but one more day on Apr 15, 2026 would make 91 in the window and break the rule.
