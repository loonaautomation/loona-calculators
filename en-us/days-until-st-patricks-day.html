When is St Patrick's Day?

St Patrick's Day on March 17 honors the patron saint of Ireland and has become a global celebration of Irish culture — parades, green clothing and shamrocks from Dublin to New York to Sydney.

Date rule: March 17 every year. In 2026, St Patrick's Day falls on Tuesday, March 17, 2026.

Facts about St Patrick's Day

  • Saint Patrick, a 5th-century missionary, is credited with bringing Christianity to Ireland; legend says he used the three-leaf shamrock to explain the Holy Trinity.
  • The first St Patrick's Day parade was held not in Ireland but in America — in St Augustine, Florida, in 1601 (and famously in Boston in 1737 and New York in 1762).
  • Chicago has dyed its river green for the day since 1962, using a vegetable-based dye.
  • It is a public holiday in Ireland and Northern Ireland; when March 17 falls on a Sunday, the Irish holiday is observed on the Monday.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is St Patrick's Day?

March 17 every year — the traditional date of Saint Patrick's death. The table shows the weekday for this year and the next three years.

Why do people wear green?

Green is Ireland's color — the 'Emerald Isle', the shamrock, and the green flag of Irish nationalism. Folklore adds that green makes you invisible to leprechauns, who would pinch anyone they could see — hence the American tradition of pinching those not wearing green.

Was Saint Patrick Irish?

No — he was born in Roman Britain and was carried to Ireland as a captive at about sixteen, later returning as a missionary bishop. He became Ireland's patron saint through his life's work there.

Is it a public holiday outside Ireland?

It is a public holiday in Ireland, Northern Ireland and the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador; elsewhere it is a widely celebrated cultural day, with some of the world's biggest parades in the United States.

What is the shamrock's role?

The three-leaf clover is said to have been Patrick's teaching tool for the Trinity — three persons in one God as three leaves on one stem. It remains Ireland's most recognizable emblem.
