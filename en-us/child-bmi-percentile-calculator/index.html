🧒 Child BMI Percentile Calculator

Enter your child's age, sex, height and weight. The calculator computes their BMI and converts it to a percentile and z-score using the official CDC growth-chart LMS data — the same reference doctors use — for ages 2 to 20.

Quick answer: Children's BMI is read as a percentile for their age and sex, not on the adult scale: below the 5th percentile is underweight, 5th to under 85th is healthy weight, 85th to under 95th is overweight, and 95th or above is obesity (CDC categories).

How to use the Child BMI Percentile Calculator

  1. Enter the child's age in years and months (age 2 to 20).
  2. Select boy or girl — the CDC charts are sex-specific.
  3. Enter height in cm and weight in kg.
  4. Read the BMI, its percentile and z-score, and the CDC category.
Example: A 10-year-old boy (120.5 months) with a BMI of 16.65 sits exactly at the CDC median — the 50th percentile. A BMI of 22.15 at the same age is the CDC 95th percentile.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where does the data come from?

The official CDC growth-chart data file (bmiagerev.csv, LMS parameters by sex and month of age). This page embeds the CDC L, M and S values every 6 months from 24.5 to 240.5 months and interpolates linearly between them; percentiles at the table's own points match the CDC published percentiles.

How does the LMS method work?

Each age/sex has three numbers: L (skewness power), M (median BMI) and S (variation). The z-score is z = ((BMI ÷ M)^L − 1) ÷ (L × S), and the percentile is the standard normal CDF of z. It is the exact method the CDC publishes.

Why not use the adult BMI categories?

Because children are still growing: a BMI that is healthy at 16 can be high at 8. Percentiles compare a child with the reference population of the same age and sex, which is why pediatric practice uses them.

Should I worry about one result?

A single percentile is a screening snapshot, not a diagnosis. Growth patterns over time matter more. Always discuss concerns with your child's doctor. This page is educational only.

How it works — the formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Then the CDC LMS method: with the L, M, S values for the child's sex and age in months (interpolated from the CDC table), z = ((BMI ÷ M)^L − 1) ÷ (L × S) and percentile = Φ(z) × 100, where Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution. Categories (CDC): < 5th underweight; 5th–<85th healthy weight; 85th–<95th overweight; ≥ 95th obesity.

Worked example

Inputs: boy, 120.5 months, BMI 22.154 (the CDC P95 value at this age, from the official table).

Steps: CDC values at 120.5 months for boys: L = −2.76565, M = 16.64614, S = 0.12011. BMI ÷ M = 22.154 ÷ 16.646 = 1.3308. (1.3308^(−2.76565) − 1) = (0.4537 − 1) = −0.5463. z = −0.5463 ÷ (−2.76565 × 0.12011) = −0.5463 ÷ −0.33222 = 1.644. Φ(1.644) ≈ 0.950.

Result: Percentile ≈ 95th — exactly the CDC published P95, confirming the method.
