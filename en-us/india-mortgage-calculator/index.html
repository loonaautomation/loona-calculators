🇮🇳 India Home Loan EMI Calculator

Work out a home loan in India: enter the property price and your down payment to see the exact monthly EMI on the loan amount, the total interest over the term and the month you become debt-free — with an extra-payment field that shows how much interest regular prepayments can save you.

How to use the India Home Loan EMI Calculator

  1. Enter the property price and your down payment percentage — the calculator borrows the rest.
  2. Enter the interest rate from your mortgage offer.
  3. Enter the term in years, add any monthly overpayment you plan to make, and press Calculate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a home loan EMI calculated in India?

Indian home loans use the reducing-balance method: interest is charged each month on the outstanding balance at the annual rate ÷ 12, and the rest of your fixed EMI reduces the principal. The EMI comes from the standard annuity formula, EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), so early EMIs are mostly interest and later ones mostly principal.

How much down payment do I need for a home loan in India?

Under RBI loan-to-value rules, banks can finance up to 90% of the property value for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for loans between ₹30 lakh and ₹75 lakh, and 75% for loans above ₹75 lakh. Your down payment is the rest — 10% to 25% of the price — and you also need separate cash for stamp duty and registration.

Are Indian home loan interest rates fixed or floating?

Most home loans in India are floating-rate, linked to an external benchmark — usually the RBI repo rate — so your EMI or your tenure changes when the benchmark moves. Fixed-rate loans exist but are less common and usually cost more. This calculator assumes one rate for the whole term, so treat the result as a snapshot at today's rate.

How it works — the formula

The monthly payment M on a loan P at monthly rate r (= annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) over n monthly payments is:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Total interest = M × n − P. With a monthly overpayment, the calculator simulates the balance month by month: interest is charged on the balance, the payment plus overpayment is applied, and the loan ends as soon as the balance reaches zero — which is why overpayments early in the term save the most.

Worked example

Example: ₹50,00,000 at 8.5% over 20 years (240 payments). r = 0.00708, so M = ₹50,00,000 × 0.00708 ÷ (1 − 1.00708−240) = ₹43,391.16 per month. Total paid = 240 × ₹43,391.16 ≈ ₹1,04,13,878.80, of which ₹54,13,878.80 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include stamp duty, registration or processing fees?

No. Stamp duty and registration (often 5–8% of the property value, varying by state), the bank's processing fee and insurance premiums are separate costs that are not part of the EMI math. If a lender adds a fee to the loan, the amount you pay interest on goes up.

Does this calculator do interest-only loans?

No — it calculates a standard reducing-balance repayment loan, where every EMI reduces the balance and the loan is cleared by the end of the term. Interest-only structures are not standard for Indian retail home loans.
