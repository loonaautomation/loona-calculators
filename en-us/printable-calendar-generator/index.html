🖨️ Printable Calendar Generator

Choose a month and year — or generate the whole year at once — and get clean, print-ready calendar grids. Pick a Monday or Sunday week start, then print or save as PDF from your browser.

Quick answer: A calendar grid needs the weekday of the 1st of the month and the number of days in it: for example November 2026 starts on a Sunday and has 30 days, so a Monday-start grid opens with six blanks and ends on Monday November 30.

How to use the Printable Calendar Generator

  1. Enter the year and choose one month — or tick Full year.
  2. Choose whether weeks start on Monday or Sunday.
  3. Press Generate, then Print for a clean paper copy (only the calendars print).
Example: November 2026, Monday start: the 1st is a Sunday, so the first row holds six empty cells then 1 on Sunday; the month runs to Monday November 30 in the sixth row's first cell.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far back and forward does it work?

Any year from 1900 to 2100 using the Gregorian calendar, which covers every practical planning need. Leap years are handled by the standard rule (divisible by 4, except centuries not divisible by 400).

Can I add my own events or notes?

Print the blank grid and write on paper — the classic approach — or print to PDF and annotate digitally. The generator intentionally produces a clean, unbranded grid.

Why does the printout only show the calendars?

Print styles hide the header, forms and buttons, so the paper copy is just the month grids with their titles.

Which countries start the week on Sunday?

The USA, Canada and Japan commonly print Sunday-start calendars; most of Europe and the UK use Monday-start (the ISO standard). Choose whichever matches your wall planner.

How it works — the formula

The grid for a month is fully determined by two facts: the weekday of the 1st and the number of days in the month (28–31, with February's length set by the leap-year rule: leap if year ÷ 4 = 0, except century years, which must divide by 400 — so 2000 was leap and 1900 was not). Blank cells before the 1st = (weekday of 1st − week-start day + 7) mod 7. Everything else is counting.

Worked example

Inputs: November 2026, Monday start.

Steps: Nov 1, 2026 is a Sunday. With Monday as day 0 … Sunday as day 6, blanks before the 1st = 6. November has 30 days (2026 is not a leap year, and November always has 30). Total cells used = 6 + 30 = 36 → 6 rows (36 ÷ 7 rounds up to 6 weeks).

Result: A 6-row grid ending with Monday November 30 in the first cell of the last row.
