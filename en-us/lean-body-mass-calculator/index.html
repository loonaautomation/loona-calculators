💪 Lean Body Mass Calculator

Estimate your lean body mass — everything that is not fat: muscle, bone, organs and water — from just your weight, height and sex, using the three standard Boer, James and Hume formulas side by side.

Quick answer: Lean body mass is estimated by formula: for example the Boer formula gives a man of 80 kg and 180 cm about 61.4 kg of lean mass. This page shows Boer, James and Hume estimates together; the Body Fat Calculator elsewhere on this site uses body measurements instead.

How to use the Lean Body Mass Calculator

  1. Select male or female.
  2. Enter your weight in kilograms and height in centimeters.
  3. Compare the three formula results — they usually agree within a few kilograms.
Example: A man of 80 kg and 180 cm: Boer gives 0.407×80 + 0.267×180 − 19.2 = 61.4 kg lean mass, James gives 62.7 kg and Hume gives 57.8 kg — treat the spread between formulas as the honest estimate range.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from the Body Fat Calculator?

That page estimates body fat from tape measurements (neck, waist, hips) using the US Navy method, then derives fat mass. This page needs no measurements: it predicts lean mass directly from weight, height and sex using the Boer, James and Hume equations, which were fitted on clinical body-composition data.

Which formula is best?

Boer is generally considered the most accurate of the three for general adults. Showing all three gives an honest range instead of false precision — all are estimates with several kilograms of possible error.

What is lean body mass used for?

Protein targets, some drug-dose calculations, and tracking whether weight change is muscle or fat. Athletes and clinicians use it as a starting estimate, then measure properly when precision matters.

Does lean body mass mean muscle mass?

No — it includes bones, organs, skin and body water as well as muscle. Skeletal muscle is roughly half of lean mass in most adults.

How it works — the formula

Boer: men LBM = 0.407W + 0.267H − 19.2; women LBM = 0.252W + 0.473H − 48.3. James: men LBM = 1.1W − 128(W ÷ H)²; women LBM = 1.07W − 148(W ÷ H)². Hume: men LBM = 0.32810W + 0.33929H − 29.5336; women LBM = 0.29569W + 0.41813H − 43.2933. In all three, W is weight in kg and H is height in cm.

Worked example

Inputs: man, W = 80 kg, H = 180 cm.

Steps: Boer: 0.407×80 + 0.267×180 − 19.2 = 32.56 + 48.06 − 19.2 = 61.42 kg. James: 1.1×80 − 128×(80÷180)² = 88 − 128×0.19753 = 88 − 25.28 = 62.72 kg. Hume: 0.32810×80 + 0.33929×180 − 29.5336 = 26.25 + 61.07 − 29.53 = 57.79 kg.

Result: Boer 61.4 kg, James 62.7 kg, Hume 57.8 kg — the spread between published formulas is itself a guide to the uncertainty, with Boer as the headline figure.

Educational estimates only. Formula results vary; clinical measurement (for example DEXA) is needed for precise body composition.
