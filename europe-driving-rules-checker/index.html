🚗 Europe Driving Rules Checker

Pick a European country to see the essentials for visiting drivers: which side of the road, the motorway speed limit for cars, the drink-drive limit, and the local rules that most often catch tourists out.

Quick answer: Most of Europe drives on the right with motorway limits of 120–130 km/h and a 0.05% drink-drive limit. The big exceptions: the UK and Ireland drive on the left, Germany has unrestricted autobahn sections, and Poland's drink-drive limit is a strict 0.02%.

How to use the Europe Driving Rules Checker

  1. Select the country you will drive in.
  2. Read the driving side, motorway limit and alcohol limit.
  3. Note the country-specific warnings before you set off — and always follow local signs, which override any guide.
Example: Driving in Poland: right-hand traffic, motorway limit 140 km/h (Europe's highest), a strict 0.02% alcohol limit, and headlights required at all times of day, all year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these the full laws of each country?

No — a traveller's summary of the headline rules for car drivers. Full law includes many more details (child seats, winter equipment dates, city zones, documentation). Local signs and police instructions always take precedence.

Why do drink-drive limits differ so much?

Each country sets its own: 0.02% in Poland, 0.05% across most of western Europe, 0.08% in England and Wales. The safe rule everywhere is simple: if you drive, do not drink at all.

Do speed limits change in rain or for new drivers?

Often yes — France and Italy cut motorway limits in rain, and several countries impose lower limits on novice drivers. The checker notes the main cases; follow the signs.

What documents should a visiting driver carry?

Passport/ID, driving licence (plus an International Driving Permit where required), vehicle registration and insurance documents, and any vignette or toll accounts for the route.

Worked example

Case: a British driver taking their car to France.

Check: Side flips from left to right. Motorway limit 130 km/h (110 in rain) instead of 70 mph. BAC limit drops from 0.08% to 0.05%. Extras: hi-vis vest in the cabin, headlamp beam adjustment, and a Crit'Air sticker if entering covered cities.

Result: Four concrete changes to prepare before the ferry — exactly what this checker summarises per country.
