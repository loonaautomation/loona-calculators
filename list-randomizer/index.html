🔀 List Randomizer

Paste any list — names, tasks, songs, raffle entries — and shuffle it into a truly random order, or draw a random sample of a chosen size from it. The list never leaves your browser.

Quick answer: A list randomizer shuffles items with the Fisher–Yates algorithm so every possible ordering is equally likely. It can also pick N random items without repeats — sampling without replacement.

How to use the List Randomizer

  1. Paste your items, one per line.
  2. Choose Shuffle whole list, or Pick a sample and set how many items to draw.
  3. Press Randomize and copy the result.
Example: A 5-item list (A, B, C, D, E) has 5! = 120 possible orders, each equally likely after a fair shuffle. Picking a sample of 2 from 10 items can produce C(10, 2) = 45 possible pairs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from the Random Team Generator and Wheel of Names?

Those turn a random order into teams or a single spinning winner. This tool's job is the raw shuffled list itself — a random running order, queue or sample — which you then use anywhere.

Is the shuffle fair?

Yes. It uses a Fisher–Yates shuffle driven by crypto-grade random numbers, the standard method that makes every ordering equally likely.

Can an item appear twice in a sample?

No. Sampling is without replacement: each drawn item is removed from the pool, like drawing names from a hat.

Is my list uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser; close the page and the list is gone.

How it works — the formula

The Fisher–Yates shuffle walks the list from the last item to the second, swapping each item with a uniformly random item at or before its position. A list of n items has n! possible orders, and Fisher–Yates gives each exactly probability 1 ÷ n!. Picking a sample of k from n is a partial shuffle: there are C(n, k) = n! ÷ (k! × (n−k)!) possible samples, all equally likely.

Worked example

Inputs: 10 items, sample size 2.

Steps: C(10, 2) = 10! ÷ (2! × 8!) = (10 × 9) ÷ 2 = 45 possible pairs. A fair pick gives each pair probability 1 ÷ 45.

Result: Any specific pair — say items 3 and 7 — has a 1 in 45 chance, the same as every other pair.
