🔢 Matrix Calculator

Enter two matrices (2×2 or 3×3) and add, subtract or multiply them — or compute the determinant and inverse of matrix A on its own. Singular matrices are reported honestly when no inverse exists.

Quick answer: For a 2×2 matrix [[a, b], [c, d]], the determinant is ad − bc, and if it is not zero the inverse is (1 ÷ (ad − bc)) × [[d, −b], [−c, a]]. This calculator also handles 3×3 matrices with cofactor expansion.

How to use the Matrix Calculator

  1. Choose the size: 2×2 or 3×3.
  2. Type the values of matrix A (and B for two-matrix operations).
  3. Pick the operation: A + B, A − B, A × B, determinant of A, or inverse of A.
  4. Read the result matrix — determinants and inverses state clearly when a matrix is singular.
Example: A = [[1, 2], [3, 4]] has determinant 1×4 − 2×3 = −2, and its inverse is [[−2, 1], [1.5, −0.5]]. Multiply A by that inverse and you get the identity matrix [[1, 0], [0, 1]].

Frequently Asked Questions

How is matrix multiplication different from adding?

Addition matches cell to cell. Multiplication combines rows of A with columns of B: each result cell is a sum of products, so A × B is generally not the same as B × A.

When does a matrix have no inverse?

When its determinant is zero — the matrix is called singular. It squashes space into a lower dimension, so the transformation cannot be undone, and this calculator reports that instead of inventing an answer.

What is the determinant in plain words?

A single number that measures how much the matrix scales areas (or volumes in 3×3). A determinant of −2 means areas double and flip orientation.

Can I do bigger matrices?

This tool covers 2×2 and 3×3, which handle the great majority of school, college and everyday engineering problems.

How it works — the formula

Determinant (2×2): det [[a, b], [c, d]] = ad − bc. Determinant (3×3): cofactor expansion along the first row. Inverse: A⁻¹ = adjugate(A) ÷ det(A), computed here by Gauss–Jordan elimination — the same result, with exact handling of the singular case (det = 0 means no inverse). Multiplication: (AB)ᵢⱼ = the dot product of row i of A with column j of B.

Worked example

Inputs: A = [[1, 2, 3], [0, 1, 4], [5, 6, 0]].

Steps (determinant): Expand along the first row: 1 × (1×0 − 4×6) − 2 × (0×0 − 4×5) + 3 × (0×6 − 1×5) = 1 × (−24) − 2 × (−20) + 3 × (−5) = −24 + 40 − 15 = 1.

Result: det(A) = 1, and since the determinant is 1 the inverse has whole numbers: [[−24, 18, 5], [20, −15, −4], [−5, 4, 1]]. Multiplying A by it gives the 3×3 identity matrix.
