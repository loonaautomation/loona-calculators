🔑 Passphrase Generator

Generate a strong password you can actually remember: a string of randomly chosen real words, Diceware-style. Choose how many words, the separator, capitalisation, and whether to add a number.

Quick answer: A 5-word passphrase drawn from this page's 160+ word list has about 160^5 ≈ 10^11 combinations before capitalisation and number options — strong because of length, yet far easier to type and remember than random characters.

How to use the Passphrase Generator

  1. Choose the number of words (4–8; 5 or 6 is a good default).
  2. Pick a separator and whether to capitalise each word or add a random number.
  3. Press Generate — press again for a fresh phrase. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere.
Example: A generated passphrase might look like: Copper-Willow-Anchor-Planet-Ember-42 — six words, capitalised, hyphen-separated, with a trailing number.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from the Password Generator on Loona Text Tools?

That tool builds character passwords (random letters, digits and symbols of a set length). This one builds word passphrases — a different, deliberately memorable format popularised by Diceware and recommended by security guidance for humans. They are separate tools for separate preferences; many people use passphrases for master passwords and character passwords in a manager.

How strong is a word passphrase?

Strength comes from randomness and word count: each word from a 160-word list adds about 7.3 bits of entropy, so 5 words ≈ 37 bits and 7 words ≈ 51 bits, before separator/case/number variations. For the highest-stakes accounts, use more words.

Are the words chosen really at random?

Yes — crypto-grade random numbers pick each word independently, with replacement, so repeats are possible and every combination is equally likely.

Should I use a passphrase I generated here for banking?

Generated phrases are never stored or transmitted, and the maths is sound — but for critical accounts, generating inside your password manager is the gold standard. This tool is ideal for learning the format and for lower-stakes logins.

How it works — the formula

Each word is drawn independently and uniformly from the embedded word list of W words using crypto-grade randomness, so a k-word phrase has W^k equally likely outcomes and entropy k × log₂(W) bits. With W ≈ 160: log₂(160) ≈ 7.32 bits per word.

Worked example

Inputs: 6 words, list size 160.

Steps: Combinations = 160^6 = 16,777,216 × 10^6 ≈ 1.68 × 10^13. Entropy = 6 × log₂(160) = 6 × 7.32 = 43.9 bits, before the extra uncertainty from capitalisation choice and the optional two-digit number (×100 → about +6.6 bits when enabled and unknown to an attacker).

Result: A 6-word phrase carries roughly 44–50 bits of entropy — strong for a memorable secret.
