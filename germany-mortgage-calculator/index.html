🇩🇪 Germany Mortgage Calculator

German mortgages work differently: you fix the interest rate for 5–30 years and choose an initial repayment rate (Tilgung); the monthly annuity stays constant while the interest share shrinks. This calculator shows your annuity, the interest paid during the fix, and the remaining debt (Restschuld) you will need to refinance.

How to use the Germany Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount in euros (Kaufpreis minus your equity/Eigenkapital).
  2. Enter the interest rate (Sollzins) from the bank's offer.
  3. Enter the initial Tilgung — 2% is a common starting point — and the fixed-interest period (Zinsbindung), then press Calculate.
Example: A €300,000 loan at 3.5% interest with 2% initial Tilgung has an annuity of €16,500.00 a year — €1,375.00 a month. After the 10-year fix, €228,283.74 of debt remains to refinance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Tilgung?

Tilgung is the repayment portion of your annuity, expressed as a percentage of the original loan per year. With 2% Tilgung on €300,000 you initially repay €6,000 of capital a year — but because the annuity stays fixed while interest falls, the repayment share grows every month, so the loan clears faster than the percentage suggests.

What is the annuity (Annuität)?

The constant annual payment: (interest rate + Tilgung) × the original loan amount. On €300,000 at 3.5% + 2%, the annuity is 5.5% × €300,000 = €16,500 a year, or €1,375 a month. Inside that fixed payment, the mix shifts from mostly interest at the start to mostly repayment at the end.

What happens at the end of the Zinsbindung?

The interest rate is only fixed for the chosen period (commonly 10 years). After that, the remaining debt (Restschuld) must be refinanced — by an Anschlussfinanzierung with the same or a new bank, at the rates of that future day. A higher Tilgung means less remaining debt and less exposure to future rate rises.

How it works — the formula

German annuity loans (Annuitätendarlehen) keep the payment constant:

Annual annuity = loan × (interest rate + Tilgung) ÷ 100  ·  Monthly payment = annual annuity ÷ 12

Each month, interest is charged on the current balance at (rate ÷ 12); the rest of the fixed payment repays capital. The calculator simulates all months of the fixed period to find the interest total and the Restschuld, and continues the same simulation to project the full repayment time.

Worked example

Example: €300,000 at 3.5% with 2% Tilgung. Annuity = 300,000 × 5.5% = €16,500/year = €1,375.00/month. First month: interest = 300,000 × 3.5% ÷ 12 = €875, repayment = 1,375 − 875 = €500. After 120 months the remaining debt is €228,283.74 and total interest paid in the fix is €93,283.74.

Frequently asked questions

How high should the Tilgung be?

Higher Tilgung = debt gone sooner and far less interest overall, but a higher monthly payment. A common rule of thumb is at least 2%, and enough that the loan would be repaid by retirement. Use the projected full term shown by this calculator to test different values — moving from 1% to 2% typically cuts many years.

What is Sondertilgung?

Many German contracts allow unscheduled extra repayments (Sondertilgung), often up to 5% of the loan per year without charge. Extra repayments reduce the remaining debt at the end of the fix; they usually do not change the monthly annuity.

Is this financial advice?

No — a free educational calculator in the German annuity style. German mortgage offers also involve the effective rate (effektiver Jahreszins), fees and property transfer taxes (Grunderwerbsteuer) that are outside this calculation; compare full offers and consider independent advice.
