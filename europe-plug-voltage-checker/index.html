🔌 Europe Plug & Voltage Checker

Pick any European country to see the plug types used there, the mains voltage and frequency — and plain guidance on whether a simple adapter is enough or a voltage converter is needed for your devices.

Quick answer: Almost all of Europe runs on 230 V at 50 Hz. The differences are the plug shapes: most of the continent uses Type C/F, France/Belgium/Poland use C/E, Italy also uses L, Switzerland uses J, and the UK and Ireland use the three-pin Type G.

How to use the Europe Plug & Voltage Checker

  1. Select the country you are visiting.
  2. Select where your devices come from.
  3. Read the plug types and voltage, and the adapter/converter verdict.
Example: Visiting France from the UK: France uses Type C & E at 230 V/50 Hz. The voltage matches the UK's 230 V, so you only need a plug adapter (UK Type G to French Type E) — no voltage converter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an adapter and a converter?

An adapter only changes the plug shape so your plug physically fits. A converter changes the voltage. If your device's label says INPUT: 100–240 V (true of virtually all phone, laptop and camera chargers), an adapter alone is safe anywhere in Europe.

Which European plugs are interchangeable?

The slim Type C (Europlug) fits C, E, F, J (usually), K and L sockets loosely-to-well, which is why one small adapter covers most of the continent. Earthed appliances with F or E plugs need the matching earthed adapter for full safety.

Why does Switzerland need its own adapter?

Swiss Type J sockets have a third pin in a unique triangle layout; Type C plugs fit, but earthed plugs from elsewhere do not. A universal adapter or a Swiss-specific one solves it.

Is the UK really the odd one out?

The UK and Ireland use Type G — the large three-pin plug with a fuse — which fits nowhere else in Europe, and European plugs fit UK sockets only through an adapter. Voltage is the same 230 V on both sides.

How it works — the formula

A curated reference lookup of national standards (IEC): voltage and frequency are effectively uniform across Europe at 230 V / 50 Hz, so the tool's real work is plug-shape compatibility: it compares the destination's plug families with your home plug family and applies the rule that a voltage converter is only needed when the home standard is 120 V (North America) and a device is not marked 100–240 V.

Worked example

Case: UK traveller (Type G, 230 V) visiting Switzerland (Type C & J, 230 V).

Check: Voltage matches (230 V = 230 V) → no converter. Plug families differ (G ≠ C/J) → adapter required, and it must be a Swiss Type J-capable one for earthed plugs.

Result: Adapter: yes. Converter: no.
