🎲 Probability Calculator

Work out probabilities for one or two events: both happening, either happening, neither, or exactly one — plus convert between probability and odds, and compute combinations C(n, r) and permutations P(n, r). The Statistics Calculator covers data summaries; this page covers event probability.

Quick answer: For two independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B), and P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). With P(A) = P(B) = 0.5: both = 0.25, either = 0.75, neither = 0.25, exactly one = 0.50.

Combinations & permutations (optional)

How to use the Probability Calculator

  1. Enter the probability of event A and event B (as decimals 0–1 or percentages — the calculator treats values over 1 as percentages).
  2. Choose whether the events are independent or mutually exclusive if you use the quick modes.
  3. Or scroll to combinations/permutations: enter n and r to get C(n, r) and P(n, r).
Example: Flipping two fair coins: P(heads) = 0.5 for each. Both heads = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25. At least one head = 0.75. No heads = 0.25. Exactly one head = 0.50.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between independent and mutually exclusive?

Independent events do not affect each other (two coin flips). Mutually exclusive events cannot both happen (rolling a 2 and a 5 on one die) — for those, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) and P(both) = 0. Events can be neither, but never both at once.

How do combinations and permutations differ?

Permutations count arrangements where order matters: P(n, r) = n! ÷ (n−r)!. Combinations count selections where order does not matter: C(n, r) = n! ÷ (r! × (n−r)!). A podium finish is a permutation; a committee is a combination.

How do I convert probability to odds?

Odds in favour = p ÷ (1 − p), written as a ratio. A probability of 0.75 is odds of 3:1 in favour, and 1:3 against.

Does this page replace the Statistics Calculator?

No. The Statistics Calculator summarises data sets (mean, median, standard deviation). This page works with event probabilities and counting — the other half of a typical probability-and-statistics course.

How it works — the formula

Independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B); P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A)×P(B); P(neither) = (1 − P(A)) × (1 − P(B)); P(exactly one) = P(A or B) − P(A and B). Mutually exclusive: P(both) = 0 and P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Counting: P(n, r) = n! ÷ (n−r)! and C(n, r) = n! ÷ (r! × (n−r)!).

Worked example

Inputs: P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.5, independent; and n = 10, r = 3.

Steps: Both = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25. Either = 0.5 + 0.5 − 0.25 = 0.75. Neither = 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25. Exactly one = 0.75 − 0.25 = 0.50. C(10, 3) = 10! ÷ (3! × 7!) = (10 × 9 × 8) ÷ 6 = 120. P(10, 3) = 10 × 9 × 8 = 720.

Result: Both 25%, either 75%, exactly one 50%; C(10, 3) = 120, P(10, 3) = 720.
