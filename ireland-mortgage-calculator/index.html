🇮🇪 Ireland Mortgage Calculator

Work out a mortgage in Ireland: enter the property price and your deposit to see the exact monthly repayment, the total interest over the term and the month you become mortgage-free — with an overpayment field that shows how much sooner you could finish, and how much interest you would save.

How to use the Ireland Mortgage Calculator

  1. Enter the property price and your deposit percentage — the calculator borrows the rest.
  2. Enter the interest rate from your mortgage offer.
  3. Enter the term in years, add any monthly overpayment you plan to make, and press Calculate.
Example: A €400,000 home with a 10% deposit (€40,000) leaves a loan of €360,000. Over 30 years at 4.0% that loan costs €1,718.70 a month, with €258,730.22 of interest over the term. Add a €200 monthly overpayment and the debt-free date moves forward noticeably — try it above.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are Irish mortgage payments calculated?

Irish repayment mortgages use standard amortisation with monthly rests: interest is charged each month on the remaining balance at the annual rate ÷ 12, and the rest of the fixed payment reduces the capital. That is the annuity formula this calculator uses.

How much deposit do I need to buy in Ireland?

Under Central Bank of Ireland rules, first-time buyers can borrow up to 90% of the property value, so the minimum deposit is 10%. Second and subsequent buyers are capped at 80%, so they need 20%. There is also a loan-to-income limit — generally 4 times gross income for first-time buyers and 3.5 times for other buyers, with a limited number of exceptions.

What mortgage term can I get in Ireland?

Terms run up to 35 years, but most lenders require the mortgage to be repaid by around age 68–70, which caps the term for older borrowers. A longer term cuts the monthly payment but adds a lot of interest over the years — the calculator shows the trade-off instantly.

How it works — the formula

The monthly payment M on a loan P at monthly rate r (= annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) over n monthly payments is:

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Total interest = M × n − P. With a monthly overpayment, the calculator simulates the balance month by month: interest is charged on the balance, the payment plus overpayment is applied, and the loan ends as soon as the balance reaches zero — which is why overpayments early in the term save the most.

Worked example

Example: €360,000 at 4.0% over 30 years (360 payments). r = 0.00333, so M = €360,000 × 0.00333 ÷ (1 − 1.00333−360) = €1,718.70 per month. Total paid = 360 × €1,718.70 ≈ €618,730.22, of which €258,730.22 is interest.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include fees, insurance or stamp duty?

No. Solicitor and valuation fees, mortgage protection and home insurance, and stamp duty on the purchase (1% up to €1 million) are separate costs that are not part of the monthly repayment maths.

Repayment or interest-only — which does this calculate?

Repayment (capital and interest), the standard for Irish residential mortgages, where every payment reduces the balance and the loan is cleared at the end of the term. Interest-only arrangements are rare for home buyers in Ireland.

Is this financial advice?

No — it is an educational calculator using your figures, provided free by Loona Automation. For advice on a real mortgage, speak to a broker regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland; your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments.
