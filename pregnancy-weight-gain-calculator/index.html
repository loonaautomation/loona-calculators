🤰 Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

Enter your height, your weight before pregnancy and your current week to see the Institute of Medicine (IOM) recommended total weight gain for your BMI category and the expected gain range for your current week.

Quick answer: The IOM recommends a total pregnancy weight gain of 12.5–18 kg if underweight (BMI under 18.5), 11.5–16 kg if normal weight (18.5–24.9), 7–11.5 kg if overweight (25–29.9) and 5–9 kg if obese (BMI 30 or more).

How to use the Pregnancy Weight Gain Calculator

  1. Enter your height in centimetres.
  2. Enter your weight before pregnancy in kilograms.
  3. Enter your current week of pregnancy (1–42).
  4. Optionally enter your current weight to see the gain so far compared with the expected range for this week.
Example: Height 165 cm, pre-pregnancy weight 60 kg (BMI 22.0, normal category) at week 28: the expected gain range for week 28 is about 6.6–9.8 kg, and the recommended total for the whole pregnancy is 11.5–16 kg.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the IOM pregnancy weight gain recommendations?

Underweight (BMI below 18.5): 12.5–18 kg total. Normal weight (18.5–24.9): 11.5–16 kg. Overweight (25–29.9): 7–11.5 kg. Obese (30 or more): 5–9 kg. These are the widely used Institute of Medicine (IOM/NAM) guidelines for a single baby.

How is the expected range for my week worked out?

The first trimester (to week 13) typically adds about 0.5–2 kg in total. From week 13 to week 40 the range is spread evenly up to the IOM total range for your BMI category, which matches the IOM weekly-rate guidance for the second and third trimesters.

What are the IOM weekly rates after the first trimester?

About 0.44–0.58 kg per week if underweight, 0.35–0.50 kg if normal weight, 0.23–0.33 kg if overweight and 0.17–0.27 kg if obese.

What if I am carrying twins?

These ranges are for a single baby. Twin pregnancies have higher IOM ranges (for example 16.8–24.5 kg at normal weight). Ask your doctor for the right range for you.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is an educational guide based on published IOM ranges. Your doctor or midwife knows your full situation and their advice always comes first.

How it works — the formula

First, pre-pregnancy BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)², which selects the IOM category and its total range. For a week w up to 13, the expected gain is the first-trimester range 0.5–2 kg scaled by w ÷ 13. For weeks 14–42: minimum(w) = 0.5 + (total minimum − 0.5) × (w − 13) ÷ 27 and maximum(w) = 2 + (total maximum − 2) × (w − 13) ÷ 27, so the range reaches exactly the IOM total at week 40 and stays capped there afterwards.

Worked example

Inputs: height 165 cm, pre-pregnancy weight 60 kg, week 28.

Steps: BMI = 60 ÷ 1.65² = 60 ÷ 2.7225 = 22.0 — Normal category, total range 11.5–16 kg. Week 28 minimum = 0.5 + (11.5 − 0.5) × 15 ÷ 27 = 0.5 + 6.11 = 6.6 kg. Maximum = 2 + (16 − 2) × 15 ÷ 27 = 2 + 7.78 = 9.8 kg.

Result: Expected gain at week 28 is 6.6–9.8 kg; recommended total 11.5–16 kg.
