📈 Rent Increase Calculator

A “10% increase” sounds small until it is turned into actual rupees — and it sounds smaller still until you see what the same increase does year after year. Enter your current rent and the increase, either as a percentage or as the new amount your landlord has quoted, and see exactly what you will pay.

How to use the Rent Increase Calculator

  1. Enter your current monthly rent.
  2. Choose how you know the increase: as a percentage (say 10%) or as the new rent amount you have been quoted — the calculator works out whichever one is missing.
  3. Set how many years ahead you want the projection.
  4. Press Calculate — the new rent, the extra monthly and yearly cost, and a year-by-year table appear.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a rent increase in percent?

Multiply your current rent by (1 + percentage ÷ 100). For a 10% increase on Rs 50,000: 50,000 × 1.10 = Rs 55,000. To find the increase amount alone, it is simply current rent × percentage ÷ 100 — here Rs 5,000 a month.

What is a normal yearly rent increase?

It varies by city and by what the tenancy agreement says. In many Pakistani and Indian cities, annual increases of 5–10% are common in private agreements; in some countries and cities the increase is capped by law or linked to inflation. Your own agreement is what counts — check the clause before accepting a figure, and use this calculator to see what the quoted percentage really costs.

How much will my rent be in 5 years?

With a steady increase it compounds: rent in year 5 = current rent × (1 + rate)⁵. At 10% a year, Rs 50,000 becomes Rs 80,530 in year 5 — over 60% more, not the 50% a simple multiplication would suggest. The year-by-year table above shows your own figures.

How it works — the formula

The new rent is the current rent multiplied by one plus the increase rate. If you only know the new rent, the percentage is worked backwards: divide the new rent by the current rent, subtract 1, and multiply by 100. For the projection, the same increase is applied again each year to that year's rent — this compounding is why a steady 10% increase grows much faster than most people expect.

Formulas: New rent = current rent × (1 + increase% ÷ 100). Increase % = (new rent ÷ current rent − 1) × 100. Rent in year n = current rent × (1 + increase% ÷ 100)n.

Worked example

Inputs: Current rent Rs 50,000; increase 10%; projection 3 years.

Steps: New rent = 50,000 × 1.10 = Rs 55,000 — an increase of Rs 5,000 a month, or Rs 60,000 a year. Year 2: 55,000 × 1.10 = Rs 60,500. Year 3: 60,500 × 1.10 = Rs 66,550.

Result: New monthly rent Rs 55,000; after three yearly increases the same home costs Rs 66,550 a month — a third more than today.

Frequently asked questions

Is the yearly increase applied to the original rent or the current rent?

In most agreements it is applied to the current rent each year, so increases compound. Some agreements instead fix the increase as a percentage of the original rent, which grows in equal steps and ends up cheaper for the tenant. This calculator uses the compounded method, the more common one — and says so openly.

Can I find the percentage if I only know the new rent?

Yes — choose “The new rent amount” above and enter both rents. The percentage is (new ÷ current − 1) × 100. For example, a rise from Rs 50,000 to Rs 57,500 is (57,500 ÷ 50,000 − 1) × 100 = 15%.
