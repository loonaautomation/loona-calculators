✈️ EU261 Compensation Checker

Check what compensation you may be owed for a delayed flight under EU regulation EC 261/2004. Enter the flight distance (or pick two airports), how late you arrived, and the reason for the delay.

Quick answer: Under EC 261/2004, an arrival delay of 3 hours or more on the airline's side can pay €250 for flights of 1,500 km or less, €400 for flights over 1,500 km and up to 3,500 km, and €600 over 3,500 km — reduced by 50% on long-haul flights arriving 3–4 hours late.

How to use the EU261 Compensation Checker

  1. Enter the flight distance in kilometres, or choose the departure and arrival airports to fill it in automatically.
  2. Enter how many hours late you arrived at your final destination.
  3. Choose the reason category for the delay.
  4. Read the compensation band that may apply to you.
Example: A 2,000 km flight that arrives 5 hours late for a technical (airline) reason falls in the €400 band: distance between 1,500 and 3,500 km, delay over 3 hours, no reduction.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does EU261 compensation apply?

Generally when your flight departs from an EU/EEA/UK airport (any airline) or arrives there on an EU/EEA/UK airline, and you arrive at your final destination 3 or more hours late for reasons within the airline's control. The UK applies its mirrored UK261 rules in the same amounts in pounds.

What is the 50% reduction rule?

On flights over 3,500 km, if the airline re-routes you and you arrive between 3 and 4 hours late, the €600 compensation can be reduced by half to €300. Arriving 4 or more hours late keeps the full €600.

What counts as extraordinary circumstances?

Events genuinely outside the airline's control — severe weather, air traffic control strikes, security risks, political instability. Ordinary technical faults and crew shortages are normally the airline's responsibility, following EU court rulings. Airlines sometimes claim extraordinary circumstances too broadly; national enforcement bodies can judge disputes.

Does a delay under 3 hours pay anything?

No fixed compensation under EC 261/2004 below a 3-hour arrival delay, though the airline still owes care (meals, communication, and accommodation for overnight delays) from shorter thresholds, and a refund/re-routing choice applies at 5 hours.

Is this legal advice?

No — it is guidance on the standard EC 261/2004 bands only. Eligibility also depends on your ticket, booking, notice of cancellation and route. Confirm with the airline, a national enforcement body, or a legal adviser.

How it works — the formula

EC 261/2004 Article 7 bands, based on the great-circle flight distance and the arrival delay at the final destination: 1,500 km or less → €250; more than 1,500 km and up to 3,500 km → €400; over 3,500 km → €600. Compensation requires an arrival delay of 3 hours or more and a cause within the airline's control. On flights over 3,500 km with an arrival delay of 3 to under 4 hours, the amount is reduced by 50% (€600 → €300). Distances between airports are estimated with the haversine great-circle formula.

Worked example

Inputs: distance 2,000 km, arrival delay 5 hours, airline's responsibility.

Steps: 2,000 km falls in the 1,500–3,500 km band → base amount €400. Delay 5 hours is over the 3-hour threshold. The 50% reduction only applies over 3,500 km, so no reduction.

Result: €400 per passenger may be payable.

Disclaimer: Guidance only based on EC 261/2004 — not legal advice. Rules checked: 8 November 2026. Actual eligibility depends on route, carrier, ticket and circumstances; extraordinary circumstances can remove the right to compensation while keeping rights to care and refunds.
